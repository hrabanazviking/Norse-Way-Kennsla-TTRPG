# Blót — Full Rule Expansion (The Norse Way, Episode 7)

## I. Grounding — Why It Is True

Our template is the saga account of the great sacrifice at Hlader, and it is worth reading whole, because every mechanic below comes out of it [Heim ch.16]: the bondes gather at the temple with ale and cattle; all blood is called *hlaut*, collected in hlaut-vessels, and sprinkled on altars, temple walls, and *the people themselves* with hlaut-staves; the flesh is boiled into savory meat for those present; the fire burns in the middle of the temple floor, and the full goblets are handed across it — first **Óðinn's goblet** for victory and power, then **Njörðr's and Freyja's** for peace and a good season, then the **braga-goblet** over which vows were sworn, then the remembrance goblet for dead friends. The chief who made the feast *blessed* the goblets and the meat. Sigurd the earl paid the whole festival's expenses out of his own hand, "which was very much celebrated" — open-handedness at the blót was itself renown.

The logic of the rite is older than the sagas: *"I ween that I hung on the windy tree… With the spear I was wounded, and offered I was / To Othin, myself to myself"* [Háv 139–141]. Even the Allfather paid to receive. *Do ut des* — I give so that you give — but the gods answer only in signs: ravens circling widdershins, a sudden fair wind, ale souring in the cup. They never appear. Canon law holds: gods act only through omens, dreams, weather.

**What is homebrew here:** the blót's structure (proper place, real offering, witnesses, goblets in order, the braga-goblet as oath-venue) is attested [Heim ch.16]. ⚠️ The three great seasonal blóts (Yule, Dísablót in spring, Sigrblót before the raiding season) follow the saga pattern, but exact historical details of ritual are debated — the *structure* (gather, sacrifice, feast, vow) is well attested, the calendar specifics are tradition, not fact. The omen table, the offering tiers, the god-specific petitions, and the boast-boldness numbers are Volmarr's (or the expansion's) game machinery.

## II. The Complete Playable Rule

### At a 5E Table

**Requirements.** A blót needs three things, all non-negotiable: (1) a **proper place** — a hof, a grove, a vé, or a burial mound; a kitchen table in a rented room is not a blót-stead; (2) an **offering of real value** — livestock, silver, a crafted thing — recorded in silver weight; (3) **witnesses**. The *goði* or eldest present leads. A blót with no witnesses is not a blót — it is littering with extra steps (expansion: the community must *see* the giving, because reciprocity is public).

**The blót-stead** (expansion — the proper places, and what each lends the rite):
- **The hof (temple):** the full liturgy — fire in the middle of the floor, kettles hung, goblets handed across the fire [Heim ch.16]. The hof's blót is the district's blót; its omen speaks for the district.
- **The grove:** older than any building. A grove-blót is quieter, older-feeling — the GM should play it with fewer words and more weather. The landvættir are nearer here (§XXV).
- **The vé (sanctuary):** marked holy ground. Violence within the vé during the rite is the gravest frith-breach the table knows (§II) — the blót does not pause for it; the blót *witnesses* it.
- **The burial mound:** the dead are present. The remembrance goblet here carries double weight: name the mound's dead truly and gain +1 Hamingja as written — the ancestors are listening closer (§XXV).

**Offering tiers** (expansion; silver weight recorded on the character sheet):

| Offering | Silver weight | Omen roll |
|---|---|---|
| **Meager** — a token, a cup of ale, words only | under ½ oz | **disadvantage** (the gods notice stinginess) |
| **Adequate** — a good beast's share, honest silver | ½–4 oz | straight roll |
| **Great** — a full ox, a horse, a crafted weapon, a king's gift | 4+ oz | **advantage** |
| **Kingly** — Sigurd-of-Hlader scale: the host pays *everything* | 20+ oz or a horse + feast | advantage, and the host gains +1 Orðstír for open-handedness (§I) |

**Hosting economics** (expansion):
- The host pays for the feast *and* the offering — this is why Sigurd's generosity was "very much celebrated" [Heim ch.16]. A blót is wealth *converted* into standing.
- Co-hosting splits the cost and shares the +1 Orðstír (great blóts only; lesser blóts grant no Orðstír for hosting, only the omen).
- A goði who leads the rite well (Charisma/Performance check, DC 13, played with real spoken words) grants the *omen roll* advantage once per blót even on an adequate offering — the gods listen to a good speaker (expansion).
- A blót performed *for* you by a hired goði works mechanically, but earns no Orðstír: the community saw your silver, not your hands.

**The rite, in order** (expansion of the saga sequence — the table plays it, not handwaves it):
1. **The gathering & the hlaut** — the offering is given; blood (or its stand-in: ale poured out, if the table prefers) is sprinkled on altar and people with the hlaut-staves. Every witness is marked. No one watches from the doorway.
2. **The feast** — the meat is eaten *by those present*. No doggy-bags for the gods; the shared meal *is* the communion. A guest who will not eat with the company insults the rite (−1 Orðstír, expansion).
3. **The goblets, in order** — Óðinn's (victory and power), Njörðr's and Freyja's (peace and good seasons), then the **braga-goblet**, then the remembrance goblet for dead friends. Each is blessed by the leader and drunk in turn. **Refusing the cup is an insult** (−1 Orðstír before witnesses, §IV).
4. **The braga-goblet round** — oaths and boasts are sworn aloud (§IX). A **boast** is rated in **boldness 1–5** when sworn (GM's call, announced: *"boldness 3"*). If the boast fails, the boaster loses Orðstír **equal to its boldness**. The GM writes every braga-goblet vow down. The table will hold you to them.
5. **The remembrance goblet** — drunk to dead friends by name (expansion). Naming the dead at the blót is ancestor maintenance (§XXV): a PC who drinks the remembrance goblet to a named ancestor and speaks one true thing about them gains **+1 Hamingja** until the next seasonal turn. The dead lend their luck to those who remember them.

**Petitioning by goblet** (expansion): the first three goblets are not decoration — each names the blót's *intent*, and the omen roll is read against it. Óðinn's goblet petitions victory and power (blóts before battle, holmgang, or perilous undertakings); Njörðr's and Freyja's petition peace and good seasons (blóts for harvest, sailing, household fortune). A blót that petitions Óðinn and rolls an ill omen (1–7) means the undertaking is *ill-starred*: the GM may spend it as one free disadvantage on a roll during that undertaking, the way Wyrd-debt is spent (§III). The gods' answer has teeth.

**The omen roll.** After the rite, the GM rolls **d20 on the omen table** below (codex's `omens.json`, expanded here). Results are *never* a god appearing. The GM interprets in-world; the players interpret at the table. A great offering grants advantage; a stingy one, disadvantage. The GM **never explains** what the omen means — that is the players' work, and arguing about it at the table is the intended play.

| d20 | The sign |
|---|---|
| 1 | Ale sours in every cup at once. Something is wrong with this plan. |
| 2 | Ravens circle **widdershins** over the stead — against the sun's road. |
| 3 | A sudden cold wind kills the fire; it will not relight till the rite is done again. |
| 4 | A dream comes to the youngest present: drowning water, a black sail. |
| 5 | The hlaut will not take — it beads and runs off the altar stone. |
| 6 | A horse shies at nothing on the road home. |
| 7 | The feast-meat is tough and joyless; laughter dies early. |
| 8 | Clouds stack like shields in the direction of your intent. |
| 9 | A raven lands on the hof roof and watches the whole rite in silence. |
| 10 | The wind drops to nothing; the smoke rises straight as a spear. |
| 11 | A stranger's dog follows the party home and will not leave — a small, strange luck. |
| 12 | The ale foams high and sweet; the cups empty laughing. |
| 13 | Ravens circle **sunwise**, calling three times. |
| 14 | A fair wind rises at dawn, straight from the gods' quarter. |
| 15 | The fire burns blue-green for a breath, then gold again. |
| 16 | An old ewe bears twins in the night after the blót. |
| 17 | A white hart — or its track — crosses your path within three days. |
| 18 | The remembrance goblet tastes of honey though none was added. |
| 19 | Two ravens land on the boundary stones and face outward, on guard. |
| 20 | The sun breaks through storm-cloud at the rite's end and strikes the altar. The season will remember this. |

(1–7: ill-omened; 8–13: ambiguous — the gods are listening but undecided; 14–20: favored.)

**Omens in campaign play** (expansion):
- Record every omen and its interpretation *in the campaign log*, with the date. The table's later arguments about what the ravens meant are the intended play — and the GM should occasionally prove an old reading right.
- An ill omen (1–7) is not a veto: it is a *warning*. Sailing anyway is allowed — and very Norse. The warning just means the GM is entitled to spend it.
- Omens are *never* retroactively explained by the GM. If the players misread the signs, the misreading stands. That is also very Norse.
- A natural 20 on the omen roll is a season-defining sign: let its effects echo for the whole season (fair winds, good hunting, the district's mood lifted).
- A natural 1 is the opposite: name it at the next Thing. The district will want to know why the ale soured.

**Boasts and vows — edge cases** (expansion):
- A boast sworn **drunk** is still binding. The ale does not excuse the mouth (§IX: the GM holds the player to the *letter*).
- A boast that proves **impossible** (the ransom fleet sank; the king is dead) is not *failed* — it is *released*, but only if the boaster speaks the release before the same witnesses and pays 1 Orðstír for the presumption. Silence keeps the debt.
- A **vow sworn over the braga-goblet** is an oath in full (§IX): while pursuing it, advantage on one check per day; breaking it is *eiðrofi*.

**What the GM writes down** (expansion — the braga-goblet ledger):
- The *exact words* of every oath and boast. Vague oaths bind vaguely (§IX) — the GM holds the player to the letter.
- The boldness rating (1–5) of every boast, announced at the swearing.
- The witnesses present. A vow with no witnesses is not a vow — it is a wish.
- The season and the blót. Old vows resurface: the GM should re-read the ledger before each seasonal turn.

**The three great blóts.** Yule (midwinter), Dísablót (spring), Sigrblót (before the summer sailing) ⚠️. A PC who **hosts or co-hosts** one gains +1 Orðstír (witnessed generosity, §I); a PC who pointedly *refuses* to attend their district's blót without cause loses 1 Orðstír (the community notices absence the way it notices a closed door). **Skipping all three in a year** without cause (outlawry, exile, war) costs 1 Hamingja as well — the luck-line thins for those who never feed it (expansion).

| Blót | Season | Petition | The saga pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Yule** | Midwinter | Peace, the year's turning, the dead remembered | The great midwinter feast; oaths sworn for the coming year |
| **Dísablót** | Spring | Fertility, the dísir, good seasons | Held as the fields wake; for peace and growth |
| **Sigrblót** | Before the summer sailing | Victory and power (Óðinn's goblet first) | Sacrifice for success in the summer's undertakings |

**A blót of real cost** clears all Wyrd-debt (§III) — but only if the offering was *great* or better, and only if the blót's petition *names* the clearing ("we give that the debt be lifted"). The Norns do not accept coupons, and they do not accept vague ones.

**Blót at a burial mound** (expansion): a blót held at a mound, with the remembrance goblet drunk to the mound's dead by name, counts as ancestor maintenance for the season (§XXV) — the two rites fold into one, as they did.

**When the blót goes wrong** (expansion):
- If the rite is interrupted by violence, the blót is *void* — the offering is wasted, the omen unrolled, and the interrupter has committed frith-breach in a vé (grave: −2 Orðstír, +1 Wyrd-debt, §II).
- If the goði misspeaks the blessings or skips a goblet, the eldest witness may *correct* the rite aloud — the blót proceeds, but the goði loses 1 Orðstír for sloppiness before the gods. The gods notice liturgy.
- If no goði or elder is present, the eldest *free person* present leads — necessity consecrates. A blót led by a child, with no adult present, is not a blót: the gods are not mocked.
- An omen of 1 (ale sours in every cup) during a *great* blót is a district event: the GM should have neighboring steadings hear of it within the week. Ill omens travel faster than good ones.

### At an OSE Table

- **Omen roll:** a great offering lets you **roll twice and take the better omen**; a stingy one, **roll twice and take the worse**. (Roll the d20 twice; the GM picks which result stands.)
- **Remembrance goblet:** +1 Hamingja as written — Hamingja is system-neutral.
- **Petitioning:** an ill-starred Óðinn-blót gives the GM one free **+2 (harder) on a roll-under check** during the petitioned undertaking.
- **Everything else is identical** — the gods take silver in any century.

### Worked Example

*Illustrative only — hypothetical rolls.* Before sailing for the summer, the party holds a Sigrblót at a grove, petitioning Óðinn's goblet for victory on the voyage. They offer a young ox and 6 ounces of silver — a **great** offering. The rite is played in order; over the braga-goblet, Astrid boasts she will bring home a king's ransom from the west — boldness 4, written down by the GM. Over the remembrance goblet, the party's eldest names her grandfather and speaks one true thing about him: +1 Hamingja until the seasonal turn.

Afterward the GM rolls the omen d20 twice (great offering): the two rolls are 4 and 15. The better stands: **15 — the fire burns blue-green for a breath, then gold again.** The table argues about it for ten minutes — exactly as intended — and reads it as favor.

Astrid's boast now hangs over the whole voyage: if she returns empty-handed, she loses 4 Orðstír before every witness who heard her swear. If the fleet is lost to storm and the boast becomes *impossible*, she may speak its release before the same witnesses for 1 Orðstír — but only if she speaks. Silence keeps the debt.

## III. Teaching Beat — What the Table Learns

Players learn *reciprocity as cosmology*: you do not pray for favors, you *exchange* with powers greater than you — and the exchange has a price list, recorded in silver weight. The omen table trains them to read the world as alive: ravens, wind, and sour ale become information, not set dressing. The braga-goblet teaches the oldest lesson at the feast — never let the ale write checks your sword has to cash — and the remembrance goblet teaches the quietest one: the dead are still at the table, and they notice who remembers.
