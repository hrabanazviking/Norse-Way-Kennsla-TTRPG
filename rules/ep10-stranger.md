# The Stranger's Reception — Full Rule Expansion (The Norse Way, Episode 10)

**Video:** `videos/ep10-stranger.mp4` · **Transcript:** `transcripts/ep10-stranger.md`
**Codex source:** `HOMEBREW_CODEX.md §XXXIII` — this file expands that section; additions the codex leaves open are labeled.

**Canon warning:** everything below this header reproduces Volmarr's §XXXIII rulings *exactly* — the 1d6 table, the class and alignment weights, the keep-most/least-extreme dice rule. These are his law for this table. The GM does not rebalance them, soften them, or add a "they take it in stride" result. Expansions are labeled and never alter the core dice.

## I. Grounding — Why It Is True

The sagas know no casual tolerance of the otherworldly. When the uncanny walks into a human settlement, the response is never a shrug — it is terror or worship, and sometimes both in the same hour. Grettir's neighbors meet the draugr Glámr — a dead shepherd who walks — with pure dread: the haunting at Thorhallsstad empties the farm of its courage long before it empties it of sheep, and no one in the district treats the walking dead as a neighbor [Grett chs.32–37]. The álfar of the Eddas are mighty and perilous beings to be placated with offerings, not befriended over ale [PE]. A shape-changer or finngálkn in the sagas draws fascination and fear in equal measure. ⚠️ *Saga evidence is anecdotal rather than systematic: the polarization is the pattern — terror at one pole, worship at the other, and nothing calm in between. The table below is Volmarr's construction on that pattern.*

This is the mechanical price of the casting law (canon §2): player characters are human, and other races are vanishingly rare. The table below is what "vanishingly rare" *costs*. A table of elves and tieflings could never feel the old fear, and the old fear is load-bearing. Rarity is not a restriction on fun; it is the price of wonder.

**What is homebrew here:** the polarization (awe and terror, no calm middle) is the saga pattern. The 1d6 table, the extremity weights, and the keep-most/least-extreme dice rule are Volmarr's rulings, reproduced exactly — they are canon for this table and are not to be "balanced" by the GM. The per-result scene guidance, the tie-break, familiarity, and unstable-Hamingja rules below are the expansion's.

## II. The Complete Playable Rule

### At a 5E Table

**Trigger.** Whenever a **visibly or known** non-human being (álfr, draugr, jotunn-kin, shape-changer, or anything the community names as not-human) enters a human community, roll **1d6 for the crowd's dominant reaction** — per *group or notable individual* — or choose by context. **There is no "they take it in stride" result. That reaction does not exist.**

**"Visibly or known"** (expansion): the trigger fires on *either* condition. *Visibly* = the nature can be seen (pointed ears bared, draugr's blackened flesh, a jotunn's stature). *Known* = the community has been told truly, by a credible voice — a goði's naming, a völva's word, the being's own admission. Rumor alone does not trigger the roll; rumor triggers *whispers*, and whispers are the GM's scene-dressing, not dice. A being *passing undetected* as human triggers nothing — until discovery (see below).

**Examples at the threshold** (expansion):
- An álfr with ears bared walking the market road: *visibly* — roll.
- A draugr whose nature the völva announces at the Thing: *known* — roll, even if it looks human.
- A whispered rumor that the new trader is jotunn-kin: neither — whispers, not dice. The GM plays the suspicion as atmosphere.
- A shape-changer living three years as a karl, discovered mid-deception: roll at +1 extremity, vengeance claim, and the story travels (see below).

| d6 | The reception |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Panic.** Doors barred, children snatched indoors, the alarm raised. Some flee; some seize weapons in terror. |
| 2 | **Terror-anger.** "Ill-omen!" Stones and spears; the goði is summoned to name what it is and what it wants. |
| 3 | **Dread-awe.** They kneel at a distance, whisper, leave offerings where it will find them. None will meet its eyes. |
| 4 | **Obsessive reverence.** They follow, beg blessings, press gifts on it, treat every word as prophecy. It cannot be alone. |
| 5 | **Possessive wonder.** They crowd and stare and touch; the thing becomes the settlement's marvel and will not be let to leave. |
| 6 | **Holy dread.** It is declared landvættir or god-sent. A vé may be raised around it — or it may be begged, firmly, to go before the spirits grow angry. |

**Rolling a settlement, step by step** (expansion — procedure for the GM):
1. **Identify the reactors.** Split the community into groups (the watch, the market crowd, the goði's household) plus notable individuals (the jarl, the völva, the loudest farmer). Roll per group/individual.
2. **Score each.** Class weight + alignment weight, exactly as ruled. When in doubt about an NPC's alignment, the GM assigns it openly — "these folk are lawful; custom is load-bearing here."
3. **Roll the dice.** (1+score) dice keep most extreme; (1+|score|) keep least extreme; 0 → one die. Roll openly — the table should see fate being weighed.
4. **Offer wary respect.** Ask each spirit-touched PC/NPC: do you attempt the save? One attempt per being per community.
5. **Play the results.** Use the scene guidance below. Contradictory results between groups are *correct* — the settlement is of several minds, loudly.
6. **Record it.** Note each group's reception. It persists until familiarity shrinks it or events change it.

**What each result does in play** (expansion — scene guidance; the dice name the weather, the GM plays it):
- **Panic (1):** the settlement is *non-functional* for the scene — trade stops, doors bar, the being cannot buy, hire, or parley. Anyone approaching the being must get past frightened armed folk first. Panic *cools* to Terror-anger or Dread-awe after one full day without incident (GM's call which).
- **Terror-anger (2):** the crowd is *mobilized against* the being. Stones first, spears if it does not leave. The goði is summoned: if the goði names it *ill-omen*, the crowd acts within the hour; if the goði names it *god-sent*, re-roll the reception once (the naming has authority — expansion).
- **Dread-awe (3):** the settlement functions *around* the being — offerings appear where it will find them, paths clear before it, no one meets its eyes. It can move freely but cannot *belong*: no hirelings, no hospitality beyond the offerings, no names exchanged.
- **Obsessive reverence (4):** the being cannot be alone and cannot leave quietly — followers dog its steps, every word is treated as prophecy (the GM should let an idle remark *become* a prophecy the settlement acts on). Gifts pressed on it are debt-tokens of a strange kind: refusing them insults the givers (−1 Orðstír among them, expansion — the community's honor is engaged too).
- **Possessive wonder (5):** the settlement will not let it leave — not by force at first, but by crowding, pleading, blocking the road with bodies and celebration. Leaving requires slipping away unseen or a firm, public refusal (which sours the wonder toward Terror-anger at the GM's discretion).
- **Holy dread (6):** the being is *declared* — landvættir or god-sent — and the declaration has legal weight: harming it is now a frith-breach against the vé (expansion: the community's naming *consecrates*). A vé may be raised around it, with offerings and a warden. Or the settlement may beg it, firmly and with gifts, to go before the spirits grow angry — refusal risks the dread curdling into Terror-anger.

**Who is reacting matters.** Each reactor has an **extremity score**, summed from two axes (Volmarr's ruling, reproduced exactly):

*By calling — how they live:*
- **Iron-minded (+1)** — Fighter, Barbarian, Rogue, Ranger, Monk (*drengr, húskarl, veiðimaðr, smiðr*). No frame for the uncanny, so it hits them harder.
- **Spirit-touched (−1)** — Cleric, Druid, Wizard, Sorcerer, Warlock, Bard (*goði, völva, seiðmaðr, skald* — the skald knows the old stories of such beings). They have seen, or sung of, stranger things.

*By orderliness — how tightly they hold the world together:*
- **Lawful (+2)** — the world has a shape, custom is load-bearing, and this thing breaks the shape. *Much* more extreme.
- **Chaotic (−1)** — the world was always strange; this is only Tuesday. A *little* less extreme.
- **Neutral** on this axis: 0.

Ordinary folk with no class and no strong alignment weight roll at **score 0** (a single honest roll) unless the GM assigns them — but note what this means: a *lawful* farming village with no adventuring classes still rolls at **+2** (3 dice, keep most extreme). The lawful farmer's terror is the default, not the exception (expansion — this is the ruling's intent, stated plainly).

**The dice rule** (Volmarr's ruling, reproduced exactly): score **above 0** → roll **(1 + score)** dice and **keep the most extreme**; score **below 0** → roll **(1 + |score|)** dice and **keep the least extreme**; score **0** → a single honest roll. So a lawful fighter (+3) rolls 4 dice and keeps the most extreme — the very pillars of his world are cracking — while a chaotic sorcerer (−2) rolls 3 and keeps the mildest, and a chaotic barbarian (+1 −1 = 0) simply rolls once, pulled both ways.

**Poles and middle** (Volmarr's ruling): the **poles** are 1 (Panic), 2 (Terror-anger), 4 (Obsessive reverence), 6 (Holy dread); the **middle** is 3 (Dread-awe), 5 (Possessive wonder). "Most extreme" = the result farthest from the middle (1 and 6 are the most extreme of all); "least extreme" = the result nearest it.

**Tie-break** (expansion): if the kept dice tie at equal extremity on *opposite* poles (a 1 and a 6), the crowd is **split** — half panics, half worships, and the settlement argues with itself, loudly, in front of the being. Do not re-roll the tie; the split *is* the result, and it is the most dangerous one: a split crowd can turn on itself.

**Wary respect.** A **spirit-touched** character may attempt a **DC 13 Wisdom saving throw** to master themselves entirely: on success they react with **wary respect** — no panic, no worship; they make the warding sign, observe, and speak carefully. They become the only ones in the settlement able to parley with the being without the crowd's hysteria poisoning the talks. (Iron-minded characters may not attempt this — they have no frame to steady themselves *with*.) A spirit-touched character may attempt the save **once per being per community** — failure means they ride the crowd's reaction like everyone else (expansion).

**This applies to player characters as well as NPCs:** when the party meets something not-human, each PC's player rolls their own reception by class — the fighter may be reaching for an axe while the seiðr-worker is already reading the signs. The player *plays* the result: a PC who rolled Panic does not get to be calm because the player is. This is the rule's sharpest tooth, and the GM should enforce it kindly but firmly.

**While revealed among humans** (ongoing, Volmarr's ruling): **disadvantage on Persuasion and Deception** (to blend in or negotiate normally); **advantage on Intimidation** (fear is the one language that always translates); **Orðstír cannot be earned** through ordinary deeds while the nature is known — the community cannot weigh the honor of what it cannot comprehend; **Hamingja behaves strangely at the GM's discretion** (the Norns wove no thread for this one).

**Unstable Hamingja** (expansion): at each session's start, the GM rolls 1d6 for each revealed non-human PC; on a 1, their Hamingja is **halved (rounded up)** for the session. The luck-line was woven for humans. A völva can *see* this instability the way she sees Wyrd-debt (§III) — and may say so aloud, which is its own kind of doom.

**Familiarity** (expansion): the reception is re-rolled for each *new* community — the next village has not heard, or does not believe it has. After a full month of peaceful contact with the same community, the GM reduces the number of dice rolled by 1 (minimum 1 die) — the uncanny becomes *known*, never *normal*. The roll itself is never skipped: calm acceptance does not exist. A being that has lived openly in one settlement for a year is a *legend* there, not a neighbor.

**The goði's naming** (expansion):
- When the crowd rolls Terror-anger (2), the goði is summoned *by the rule itself* — the table should play this scene, not skip it.
- The goði names what the being *is* and what it *wants*, before witnesses. The naming is a judgment, and the goði's Orðstír rides on it.
- If the goði names it **god-sent**, re-roll the crowd's reception once (the naming has authority).
- If the goði names it **ill-omen** and the being stays, the goði may demand it leave — refusal is a frith-breach against the community's peace.
- A goði who names *falsely* (declares god-sent what is merely strange, for profit or fear) gains 2 Wyrd-debt when it comes out — the gods notice mislabeling (§III).

**Beings that return** (expansion):
- A being that leaves a community and later returns is *re-rolled* — but with one fewer die than its first reception there (minimum 1), as familiarity. The settlement remembers; memory softens, never erases.
- A being that left under Terror-anger and returns *uninvited* rolls at **+1 extremity** — the crowd remembers the stones it threw, and its arm remembers the throwing.

**The uncanny at the Thing** (expansion):
- The Thing is the assembly of *free humans* (§VII). A revealed non-human cannot bring suit, swear oaths, or stand as oath-helper — the law does not know what to do with it, which is itself the teaching.
- It *may* be spoken for: a human patron (a goði, a jarl, a wary-respect spirit-touched) can carry its cause before the assembly — at the patron's own risk. The crowd's reception applies to the *patron* by association for the session (GM's call on the extremity).
- A being that disrupts the Thing is not fined — it is *named* ill-omen and driven out, and the driving-out is lawful force, not feud-fodder. The law protects itself first.

**Mixed parties** (expansion):
- A party traveling *with* a revealed non-human is judged by association: while the being's nature is known, the whole party suffers **disadvantage on Persuasion** (5E) / **−2 on reaction rolls when parleying** (OSE) with communities that have rolled — the crowd's reaction poisons the humans' words too.
- The party's *own* receptions were rolled when they first met the being (§XXXIII, PCs included) — and they stand. A PC who rolled Terror-anger at their álfr companion does not get to be comfortable three sessions later without the familiarity rule doing its work.
- Splitting the party helps: humans who arrive *without* the being are received as humans. The sagas know this tactic — the strange companion waits in the woods while the humans do the talking. It is not cowardice; it is diplomacy.

**Undetected passing** is a different matter — spies and álfar in disguise are saga fodder, and no roll is made while the disguise holds. But **discovery mid-deception** is treated as a **níð-worthy betrayal** (§XVII): the reception is rolled immediately at **+1 extremity** (expansion: betrayal sharpens every reaction), the deceived community gains a vengeance claim, and the being's *next* reception in any community starts one step more extreme (the story travels ahead — expansion).

### At an OSE Table

- The 1d6 table, the extremity weights, and the most/least-extreme dice rule are **identical** — the table is system-neutral, and OSE's Lawful/Neutral/Chaotic needs no conversion.
- **Classes:** iron-minded = **Fighter, Barbarian, Thief, Ranger**; spirit-touched = **Cleric, Druid, Magic-User, Illusionist, Bard** (OSE Advanced Fantasy). A 5E Monk at an OSE table counts as iron-minded — a martial-discipline calling with no frame for the uncanny (GM's call, expansion).
- **Wary respect:** **Save vs. Spells** (instead of the DC 13 Wisdom save), spirit-touched only, once per being per community.
- **While revealed:** **−2 on reaction rolls** when parleying or blending in, **+2 on reaction rolls** when threatening; **no Orðstír earned** while the nature is known; Hamingja unstable as written.
- **Everything else is identical** — the uncanny is an event in any century.

### Worked Example

*Illustrative only — hypothetical rolls.* An álfr-woman, revealed and known, walks into a market town. The GM rolls reception per group.

The town watch (six fighters, Lawful): extremity +1 (iron-minded) +2 (lawful) = **+3** → roll 4d6, keep the most extreme. The dice come up 2, 3, 5, 6 — the 6 is farthest from the middle: **Holy dread**. The watch kneels in the street, and their captain begs her, firmly and with gifts, to leave before the landvættir grow angry.

The market crowd (commoners, Lawful, no class): +0 +2 = **+2** → 3d6, keep most extreme: 1, 4, 4 → **Panic** — stalls overturned, children snatched indoors, the alarm bell ringing while the watch kneels. The town is now of two minds, loudly.

One spirit-touched hedge-wise woman (Neutral): score −1 → 2d6, keep least extreme. She also attempts wary respect: DC 13 Wisdom save, rolling 11 + 3 = 14 — **success**. She makes the warding sign, steps through the panicking crowd, and speaks carefully to the álfr-woman — the only parley in the town not poisoned by hysteria. If the álfr-woman stays the month, the dice shrink — but the roll never goes away.

## III. Teaching Beat — What the Table Learns

The player learns what the sagas assumed: in this world, the uncanny is never furniture. It is always an *event*. A table of elves and tieflings could never feel the old fear, and the old fear is load-bearing — which is why the casting law exists, and why this episode's mechanics refuse to soften it. The lawful farmer's terror teaches the deepest lesson of all: to these people, the world *has an order*, and the uncanny is what happens when the order breaks. The extreme reaction is not bigotry — it is cosmology. When the PCs themselves roll their own reception and the fighter's player watches their character reach for an axe they didn't choose to reach for, the lesson lands in the body, not the notebook — which is exactly where the sagas always aimed.
