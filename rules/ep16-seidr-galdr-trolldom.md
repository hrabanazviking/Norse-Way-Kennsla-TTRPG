# Seiðr, Galdr, Trolldom — Full Rule Expansion (The Norse Way, Episode 16)

## I. Grounding — Why It Is True

The threefold division is not a game convenience — it is how the sources present magic. Seiðr is the
art of the *völva* and, in the mythic register, of Freyja herself: Völuspá's seeress declares she
knew the art, and the tradition ties it to trance, chanting, and the high seat from which the witch
spoke [PE: Völuspá]. For men it was poisonously gendered. Loki's taunt to Óðinn in *Lokasenna* —
*"with spells in Samsey once, like witches with charms didst thou work… unmanly thy soul must seem"*
— shows that even the All-father could be shamed for it [PE: Lokasenna 24]. The historical register
is grimmer: King Haraldr burned his own son Ragnvald with eighty warlocks, "which work was much
praised" [Heim] — male witchcraft could be answered with fire. The ergi-taint cost in this system is
Volmarr's mechanical translation of that recorded dread.

Galdr, by contrast, is the *respectable* art: runes carved *and sung*, protection and healing and
victory, the province of chieftains and goðar. Sigrdrífa's rune-catalogue — victory-runes,
ale-runes, birth-runes, wave-runes — describes each rune as a practical working with rules of use
[PE: Sigrdrífumál]. And the myth gives galdr its deepest law: Óðinn hung nine nights on the tree and
*paid* for the runes with himself — magic-as-price-paid is the foundation of the art [PE: Hávamál
138–141]. The three steps of Volmarr's *Galdrabók* — **Intent → Ritual → Incantation** — are his
codification of that principle: nothing here is free, silent, or instant.

Trolldom, the hearth-magic of wards and workings, is drawn from Volmarr's own trolldom chart
(purification *hreinsa*, wards *hlífðarverk*, road-opening *vegaopnun*, prosperity charms) — that
chart is canon for this table, and the domestic scale of its effects is deliberate: the wise-woman's
art protected the farm, not the battlefield. The absolute taboo on coercion, with its
fylgja-backlash and Wyrd-debt accrual, is Volmarr's system-wide law (§III, §XVIII) — historically
grounded in the culture's terror of the hidden knife and the binding of wills, mechanically his
invention.

## II. The Complete Playable Rule

### At a 5E Table

**Shared law — Intent → Ritual → Incantation (all three arts).** No silent, instant casting, ever.
Every working requires all three steps, declared in order: the practitioner states intent aloud
(even if alone), performs the ritual (minutes to hours, per the art), and speaks/sings the
incantation (audible — whispered if secrecy is the intent, but never silent). Materials are required
and consumed or dedicated: carved staves, herbs, and a real offering (silver of real weight, blood,
or a thing of value — see Blót §IV for the gift-logic). Witnesses *or* deliberately chosen solitude:
a working done in hiding gains nothing extra, and a hidden working discovered is judged exactly as
harshly as a public one — secrecy buys no protection from the consequences.

**The coercion taboo (absolute).** Any working that overrides, binds, or coerces another's will —
love-binding, thought-reading against resistance, compelled obedience, cursing a named target — is a
taboo act. Mechanically:

- **All coercive magic accrues +1 Wyrd-debt** (§III), success or failure. The debt is silent and cumulative; at 5, the Norns come calling.
- **The caster makes a Wisdom saving throw** (DC set by the severity of the coercion: 12 for a minor compulsion, 15 for binding a will, 18 for soul-level violation). On failure, the working **rebounds** — the GM describes the fylgja's price. Rebound effects are never trivial: the compulsion turns on the caster, the target's fetch-guardian visits the caster in dream and takes something (a point of Hamingja, a memory, a scar), or the gods answer with an omen of disfavor.
- **The target may also resist** with a Wisdom save (DC = 8 + caster's proficiency + caster's Charisma or Wisdom modifier) — but the target's resistance does not cancel the caster's taboo save. Coercing and failing is still coercing.
- **Edge cases:** coercing a willing volunteer (a healing trance entered freely) is not coercion. Coercing an animal or beast is minor — DC 12, no Wyrd-debt unless the beast is someone's sworn companion. Coercing the already-dead (§XXV) is grave — DC 18, always +1 Wyrd-debt. Group coercion (binding many wills at once) raises the DC by 2 per additional target beyond the first.

**Seiðr — the witch's art.** Not a class feature; it is *learned* from a practitioner at great cost:
a quest or service for the teacher, a worthy payment (no less than a season's keep), and weeks of
study. The learner emerges with the art but not the standing.

- **Effects:** divination (the future, answered in riddles — coordinate with §XXIV, never a plan), fate-nudging (one ally gains advantage on one roll within the next day, chosen by the seiðr-worker), weather-working (a storm hesitates or a wind rises — one step on the GM's weather scale), spirit-counsel (a brief converse with a summoned spirit or the dead, who answer in their own idiom), cursing (a named target suffers −1 Hamingja until rites of cleansing — cursing is coercion and always triggers the taboo above).
- **The trance** requires the high seat (*seiðhjallr* — raised platform), at least two chanting assistants, and a full hour. Interrupting the trance breaks it; the working is lost and the materials spent.
- **The ergi-taint (men):** each *public* seiðr-working costs −1 Orðstír — the community sees, and the community remembers — or −3 if mocked in flyting afterward (§X). A *private* working costs nothing unless discovered (and secrets at a Thing have a way of surfacing). A man whom the community already honors as ***seiðmaðr*** is exempt: this is a GM-granted title, earned after at least three honored workings acknowledged publicly, and it is itself saga-worthy status. **Women practitioners** (*völva*, *seiðkona*) are exempt from the taint entirely: true prophecies earn +1 Orðstír each, but NPCs keep their distance — hospitality is given warily (§VIII), and invitations are politely rare.

**Galdr — the rune-songs.** The "wizardry" of this world, deliberately not D&D wizardry. Each
practitioner begins with **3 rune-songs** and learns more only from teachers or grimoires — one week
of study per song, and the teacher names their price. Each song = one rune + one narrow effect.
Casting = carve the rune (a material focus: wood, stone, or flesh — the carving takes the full
minute), chant aloud (audible, 1 minute), and make a **Charisma or Wisdom check** (the caster's
choice): DC 12 for common workings, DC 15 for strong ones, DC 18 for the exceptional. A song may be
sung once per target per day — the runes do not repeat themselves for the impatient.

- ***Týr* (victory in lawful combat):** one ally gains advantage on their first attack roll of the next fight, provided the fight is lawful (holmgang, open battle — not ambush).
- ***Bjarkan* (healing):** one wounded creature regains 1d6 + caster's Wisdom modifier hit points. Once per creature per day.
- ***Naud* (enduring hardship):** one ally gains advantage on their next Constitution save against cold, hunger, or exhaustion.
- ***Sól* (sight):** the caster sees truly through one deception or darkness for the next hour — advantage on one Wisdom (Insight) or (Perception) check.
- ***Fé* (prosperity):** a farm, ship, or venture gains advantage on its next seasonal or voyage check (§XV) — the rune blesses honest work, not theft.
- ***Íss* (stilling):** one raging or panicked creature must make a Wisdom save (DC = caster's check result) or be calmed for 1 minute. Used on an unwilling intelligent target, this is coercion.
- **Effects are subtle and bounded:** no fireballs. A wound knits, a storm hesitates, a foe's courage falters. Galdr is socially *respectable* — goðar practice it openly, and no Orðstír cost attaches to honest rune-singing.

**Trolldom — the hearth art.** Anyone may learn it; the wise-woman's and householder's craft. Small
workings, small costs — herbs, a smoking bowl, a carved door-ward, an hour of the evening:

- **Purification smoking (*hreinsa*):** a dwelling is cleansed of ill-feeling; all within gain advantage on their next save against fear or disease within the day.
- **Ward-stave (*hlífðarverk*):** carved over a door or cradle; the first hostile act against the warded place within a week suffers disadvantage (GM's call on what counts — the ward is not a wall).
- **Prosperity bowl (*gæfa*):** set out at the year's turning; the household gains advantage on one seasonal check that year (§XV).
- **Untroubled sleep:** one night of dreamless rest — no draumr rolls (§XXIII), for good or ill, and one level of exhaustion may be shaken off without a long rest's full benefit.
- **Cost is material and time, never spiritual** — but a trolldom-worker who meddles in seiðr's business (curses, love-coercion, spirit-binding) is **judged as a seiðr-worker**: the coercion taboo, the ergi-taint, and the community's fear all apply in full.

### At an OSE Table

- **Casting law identical:** Intent → Ritual → Incantation, minutes to hours, materials, witnesses or chosen solitude.
- **Coercion:** the caster makes a **Save vs Spells** (with a −2 penalty for soul-level violations, −1 for binding a will; minor compulsions unmodified). On failure the working rebounds — the fylgja's price, described by the GM. The target's resistance is a **Save vs Spells** against the caster. All coercion accrues **+1 Wyrd-debt**, identical to 5E.
- **Seiðr identical:** learned, not class-granted; the hour-long trance with high seat and chanting assistants; the male ergi-taint (−1 Orðstír per public working, −3 if flyting-mocked; *seiðmaðr* exemption); *völva* gains and wary hospitality. Fate-nudging grants **−2 on one roll-under check** within the next day.
- **Galdr:** 3 rune-songs at start; carve + chant aloud 1 minute + **1d20 ≤ Charisma or Wisdom** (common workings at the score, strong at −2, exceptional at −4). *Týr*: one ally rolls their first attack twice, keeping better; *Bjarkan*: heal 1d6 + Wisdom modifier hp, once per creature per day; *Naud*: −2 on the next save vs cold, hunger, exhaustion; *Sól*: −2 on one check to pierce deception; *Fé*: −2 on one seasonal/voyage check; *Íss*: target Saves vs Spells or is calmed 1 turn (unwilling = coercion). Subtle and bounded, exactly as codex.
- **Trolldom identical:** purification (one reroll on a save vs fear or disease that day — or +2, table's choice); ward-stave (the first hostile act within a week is made at −2); prosperity bowl (−2 on one seasonal check); untroubled sleep (no draumr, shake one exhaustion level). Meddling in seiðr's business = judged as a seiðr-worker, either system.

### Worked Example

A 5E example (illustrative). A PC, a man of the district, seeks out a *seiðkona* to learn
weather-working — he wants to still a storm threatening the fleet. He serves her a season as agreed
(the learning cost, played out), and she teaches him the art. When the storm comes, he sets up the
high seat on the shore with two chanting assistants and works the trance for an hour (witnesses: the
whole fleet's crews). The storm hesitates — the GM gives the fleet advantage on its storm-saves. But
the working was *public*, and he is a man: −1 Orðstír for the ergi-taint (someone mutters in the
hall that night). Later, a rival mocks him in flyting for "witch's work" — the table judges the
mockery landed, so −3 instead of −1. Three such honored workings, acknowledged before the district,
and the GM may name him *seiðmaðr* — after which the taint no longer attaches. At an OSE table the
same scene plays identically, with the trance and the costs unchanged.

## III. Teaching Beat — What the Table Learns

Players learn that magic had a **social price tag** and a **gendered shape** — and that the most
powerful magic in the Norse world was the kind a wise man was *ashamed* to practice. The ergi
mechanic teaches more about Norse masculinity in one session than any lecture, because it is not a
lecture: it is a −1 on a public sheet, witnessed by the table, and the player feels the culture's
hand. They also learn the Galdrabók's deeper lesson — that power here is *never* free, silent, or
instant — and they learn to fear the coercion taboo the way the culture feared it: not as a rule,
but as a debt that follows you home.
