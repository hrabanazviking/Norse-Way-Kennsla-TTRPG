# Hamingja — Full Rule Expansion (The Norse Way, Episode 3)

**Video:** `videos/ep03-hamingja.mp4` · **Transcript:** `transcripts/ep03-hamingja.md`
**Codex source:** `HOMEBREW_CODEX.md §XVI` — this file expands that section; additions the codex leaves open are labeled.

## I. Grounding — Why It Is True

In the sagas, luck is not chance — it is a **quality of the soul**, visible to those who know how to look. Kings and great chieftains are said to have mighty *hamingja*; when a man's luck turns, the saga says so plainly, and his friends grow quiet — because a luckless man drags others down with him [Grett; Heim; Njál]. Hamingja could be **inherited**, could follow a family, could *leave*. It favored the bold, the generous, the oath-keepers, and it fled the coward, the miser, the oath-breaker. *Gipta* — good fortune — was spoken of the way we speak of health: a real thing, cultivated or squandered. The sagas even show luck passing between people: men married into lucky families, and a chief's fortune covered his followers like a cloak.

What is **Volmarr's homebrew invention** and what is attested history? The living-luck concept is thoroughly historical — the inherited luck-lines, the luck that abandons the coward, the sagas' plain statements that this man was *gæfusamr* (lucky) and that one not. The *numbers* — the 0–10 scale, all PCs beginning at 4, one point spent to reroll, the +1-to-death-saves aura — are Volmarr's invention. No saga gives luck hit points. But the rule reproduces the culture's causal logic exactly: luck is not what happens to you, it is *what you are*, and what you are is built from conduct.

## II. The Complete Playable Rule

### At a 5E Table

**Tracking.** Each PC has **Hamingja 0–10**. All begin at **4** — unproven, unspoiled. Hamingja is **semi-secret**: the GM tracks it openly, but in-world no one *announces* their luck. NPCs judge it by watching you. *(Addition to the codex: the table may know the number, but PCs should never say it aloud in character — "My luck is 7" is as wrong for the period as "my hit points are 34." Describe it: bright-eyed, ill-starred, fortune's favorite, dogged.)*

**Spend — the reward made tangible.** Before any d20 roll is resolved, you may **spend 1 Hamingja to reroll it and take the new result** — this is the universe leaning toward you. You may do this **after seeing the roll but before the outcome is declared**. *(Clarifications as additions to the codex:)*
- The reroll **replaces** the roll — you take the new result, better or worse. Luck is a living thing, not a calculator; the codex's wording ("take the new result") is the rule.
- *(Table variant:)* if your table prefers the gentler OSE-file phrasing ("take the better"), play it so — but name it as a variant, because the risk is the point.
- One Hamingja spent per roll, one reroll per roll — luck cannot be stacked on a single throw.
- You may spend to reroll **a foe's d20** too, forcing *them* to take the new result — fortune's cloak covers you and slips from your enemy. (The foe spends nothing; it is *your* luck at work.)
- A natural 20 rerolled this way may come up worse — the gods have a sense of humor, and the table must feel the risk to respect the price.

**Gain +1** (announced by the GM as *"your luck grows"* — never silently; the luck-line is public in its effects):
- Winning a battle you entered freely.
- A generous gift given openly (§VI).
- Keeping an oath at real cost (§IX).
- Fulfilling a sumbel-vow (§XIX).
- Honoring the landvættir or the dead properly (§XXV).
- Heeding a true dream (§XXIII).
- Composing a praise-poem for another (§XX).

*(Additions to the codex:)* Each distinct qualifying act earns +1; the GM may award at most **+2 per session** to prevent farming. Unlike Orðstír, Hamingja needs **no audience** — the universe saw it — but the act must be *real*: cost paid, not cost performed. A "generous gift" of copper to a beggar while keeping the silver earns nothing and everyone at the table knows it.

**Loss −1** (announced as *"your luck thins"*):
- Cowardice before witnesses.
- Breaking an oath.
- Theft by stealth.
- Killing one who has yielded.
- Ignoring a true dream.
- Insulting the landvættir.
- Miserliness at the feast-table.

**At 0 — luckless.** Once per session, the GM may *force* you to **reroll a success and take the new result** — fortune itself has turned its face away. NPCs sense it: **disadvantage on Charisma checks to recruit followers or secure trust** until Hamingja rises above 0. *(Addition to the codex:)* A luckless PC's forced reroll should be described, not hidden — "your foot finds the one loose stone" — so the table learns the shape of a turned back.

**At 8+ — luck-bright.** Allies within **30 feet** gain **+1 to their first death saving throw of the day** — men *want* to stand near the lucky, as the sagas say they did. *(Addition to the codex:)* The aura does not aid the luck-bright one themselves, and it ends the moment their Hamingja drops below 8. NPC hirelings will follow a luck-bright PC for standard pay where they would otherwise demand hazard rates — the codex's logic extended to the morale economy.

**Bequest.** A dying character may **bequeath remaining Hamingja to kin, foster-kin, or sworn companions, point for point**. *(Addition to the codex:)* The bequest must be spoken aloud — a dying breath, a last word — before witnesses; an unwitnessed bequest does not carry. Luck, like land, is inherited — which is why men married into lucky families (§XXVI).

### At an OSE Table

Identical. Spend 1 Hamingja → reroll any d20 and take the new result, or force a foe to reroll. At 0: the GM forces one reroll of a success per session; −2 on reaction rolls to recruit or earn trust. At 8+: allies within 30 feet gain +1 to their first death save of the day. Rerolls are system-agnostic — the universe leans the same in every century.

### Worked Example

Bjorn (Hamingja 6) rolls to shove open a storm-swollen gate while the draugr closes in: the die shows 4. Before the GM declares the outcome, Bjorn says *"I spend one of my luck"* — dropping to 5 — and rerolls: a 16. The gate gives. Two scenes later he has entered battle freely and won; the GM announces *"your luck grows"* — back to 6. Had he spent down to 0 instead, the next session the GM might force him to reroll a hard-won success. *(Illustrative numbers; real dice land where they land.)*

## III. Teaching Beat — What the Table Learns

Players stop treating luck as the dice and start treating it as **conduct**. The cautious, selfish PC watches their rerolls dry up while the generous oath-keeper's pile grows — and the table learns, in its bones, why the sagas call some men "lucky" the way we'd call them "good." The reroll-before-resolution timing teaches the rest: luck is not what saves you after disaster, it is what *leans* — a small, living edge, spent in the breath before the outcome lands.
