## I. Grounding — Why It Is True

This section is not sanitized, because the source is not. In *Lokasenna*, Loki taunts Óðinn himself:
*"they said you practiced seiðr on Samsø, and beat on the drum as witches do — in the likeness of a
wizard you journeyed among men, and that I thought was ergi"* [PE: Lokasenna 24]. *Ergi* meant
unmanliness in its fullest, ugliest sense: cowardice in battle *and* sexual shame — the passive, the
yielding, the perverse as the culture defined it. It was the one accusation a man could not let
stand. The law treated the ergi-words (*ragr*, *argr*, and the acts they implied) as the deepest
insults known ⚠️ — the taboo's roots run older than the law-codes that record them (the 13th-century
codes are later than our period, but the dread is older).

Note the subtlety the sagas preserve: Óðinn practiced seiðr and *remained* Óðinn. The shame lay not
in the art but in *how and why* a man used it — for cowardly, coercive, underhand ends (§V). This
file treats ergi strictly as the culture's own taboo category — a historical fact about how the 9th
century policed masculinity — and keeps everything non-explicit: the acts are named as the culture
named them (fleeing, hiding, coercing, refusing the lawful fight), never lingered on.

## II. The Complete Playable Rule

### At a 5E Table

**Ergi-accusation is a formal flyting charge** (§X). It may be leveled only **in public, before
witnesses, naming the act**. The chargeable acts, as the culture defined them:

- **Fleeing battle and leaving comrades** — witnessed flight, abandoning the shield-line.
- **Refusing holmgang when lawfully challenged** (§XI) — the lawful duel refused is cowardice made formal.
- **Using seiðr or trolldom to coerce rather than to counsel** (§V) — binding a will, cursing unseen, unmanning a rival by hidden means.
- **Striking from hiding what should be faced openly** — the night-knife, the ambush on an unsuspecting man, the poison in the cup.

A *vague* accusation — naming no act — is itself answerable: if the accusation is proven false or is
made without a named act, the **accuser** takes **1 níð-mark** for weaponizing the culture's deepest
shame (§XVII). The form protects the charge's gravity.

**The accused must answer within the scene** — by deed, by holmgang, or by counter-flyting:

- **By deed:** the accused meets the charge with action before the witnesses — takes up the challenge, rights the wrong openly, does the brave thing the accusation says he would not do. The GM and the table must agree the deed answers the charge; a token gesture is a weak answer.
- **By holmgang (§XI):** the accused demands the island-duel, there and then or at the appointed time. Demanding it *is* the answer — the fight's outcome is separate, but the courage to demand it washes the charge in the moment.
- **By counter-flyting (§X):** the accused answers word with word — best of three spoken exchanges, the table judging the stronger. The insult must be spoken aloud, in character; dice alone cannot speak it. If the accused's flyting is judged the stronger, the accusation dies. If it is the weaker, the penalties below apply *and* the accusation stands proven in the hearers' eyes.
- **Silence, flight, or a weak answer = 1 níð-mark + −2 Hamingja, immediately.** The table witnesses it; shame, like honor, needs an audience. There is no "later" — the scene *is* the court.

**A *proven* ergi-act** (witnessed cowardice, witnessed coercive magic — proof before the community,
not rumor): **1 níð-mark, −2 Orðstír, −1 Hamingja**, and the word follows the character — NPCs who
have heard it react with **open contempt until atonement**. Open contempt is mechanical: the marked
character has disadvantage on Charisma (Persuasion) checks with those who know, and no one will
stand as their oath-helper at the Thing.

**Atonement** (until atonement, the contempt stands): the marked character must perform a great deed
before witnesses, win holmgang vindication (§XI), or fulfill a sumbel-vow of conspicuous courage
(§XIX) — the table must witness the deed that washes the word, and the GM sets the price in blood,
silver, or poetry (§XVII). Atonement removes the contempt and *one* níð-mark; further marks require
further deeds.

**The seiðr nuance (§V).** The table — and the GM, *publicly* — judges intent, because the culture
did. A man practicing seiðr for **prophecy, healing, or counsel at need** suffers **no ergi-taint**;
a man using it to **unman a rival, bind another's will, or strike unseen** *does*. Practical
guidance for the judgment:

- Divining the fleet's weather before sailing: no taint.
- Healing a wounded kinsman by trance: no taint.
- Spirit-counsel sought openly for the district's good: no taint.
- Cursing a named rival from the high seat: taint — and the coercion taboo (§V) on top of it.
- Binding a rival's courage before a duel: taint — and cowardice besides.
- The GM announces the judgment aloud, with the reasoning, in front of the table. No private rulings on public shame.

**Who may accuse, and where.** Any free person may level the charge — it is not the privilege of
rank — but it must be public: a hall, a feast, a Thing, a battlefield with witnesses. An accusation
whispered in private is gossip, not law, and treating it as law is itself níð-worthy. Women of the
community may level it too — and a man mocked for *being accused by a woman* has no recourse but
answering; the culture's cruelty here is the lesson.

### At an OSE Table

- The ergi-accusation's form is **identical** — public, before witnesses, naming the act; a vague or false accusation earns the **accuser** 1 níð-mark, either system.
- Answer within the scene — by deed, by holmgang (§XI), or by a stronger counter-flyting — or take **1 níð-mark and −2 Hamingja**, immediately; níð-marks, Orðstír, and Hamingja are system-neutral currencies.
- Counter-flyting at an OSE table: best of three, each round both roll **1d20 ≤ Charisma**, the insults spoken aloud in character; the table judges the stronger — the dice support the words, they do not replace them.
- A proven ergi-act: **1 níð-mark, −2 Orðstír, −1 Hamingja**, with open NPC contempt until atonement — **identical**; contempt = **−2 on reaction rolls** with those who know, and no oath-helper will stand for the marked.
- Atonement **identical**: a great deed before witnesses, holmgang vindication, or a sumbel-vow of conspicuous courage — witnessed by the table, priced by the GM; removes the contempt and one níð-mark.
- The seiðr nuance and the GM's *public* judgment of intent are **identical** at an OSE table — the customs officers work every shift. The practical guidance (prophecy/healing/counsel = no taint; unmanning/binding/striking unseen = taint) carries over unchanged.
- Full dual-system reference: `SYSTEM_VARIANTS.md` (Appendix — Codex §XVIII).

### Worked Example

A 5E example (illustrative). In the hall, a rival stands and charges a PC warrior with ergi before
the whole feast: he names the act — "you fled the shield-line at Hrafnfjörðr and left your brother's
son to die." The accused must answer *now*. He chooses counter-flyting: three rounds of spoken
insults, each side rolling Charisma (Performance). Say the accused rolls 14 + 5 = 19 against the
rival's 8 + 3 = 11 in the deciding round, and the table judges his final verse the stronger — the
accusation dies, and the rival's standing takes the wound instead. Had the accused stayed silent, he
would have taken 1 níð-mark and −2 Hamingja on the spot, and the hall would have remembered. Had
witnesses *seen* him flee, the charge would be proven regardless of words: 1 níð-mark, −2 Orðstír,
−1 Hamingja, and open contempt until he atones — perhaps by demanding holmgang against the next man
who repeats it. At an OSE table, the flyting rounds are 1d20 ≤ Charisma, and the penalties are
identical.

## III. Teaching Beat — What the Table Learns

Players encounter a moral world that is not theirs — where courage is gendered, where *how* you
fight matters as much as *whether* you win, and where the worst thing is not cruelty but *shameful*
cruelty. The discomfort is the lesson: the 9th century is a foreign country, and the mechanics are
its customs officers. The accusation procedure teaches something subtler still — that this culture
*formalized* its cruelty. Ergi was not a slur thrown in anger; it was a legal charge with forms,
witnesses, and defenses, and the table learns to handle it the way the hall did: publicly,
seriously, and at their peril.
