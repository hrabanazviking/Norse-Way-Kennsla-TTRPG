## I. Grounding — Why It Is True

The Norse did not share one afterlife — they shared a **geography of the dead** [SnE; PE:
Grímnismál]. The battle-slain chosen by Óðinn go to **Valhöll** — spear-raftered, shield-roofed,
*"five hundred doors and forty there are, eight hundred fighters through one door fare"* when they
go to war with the wolf [PE: Grímnismál 23]. Freyja takes her chosen to **Fólkvangr** — *"the ninth
is Folkvang, where Freyja decrees"* the seating of the slain [PE: Grímnismál 14]. The drowned fall
into **Rán's net** — and sailors' offerings to Rán are an attested custom, which is why the codex
gives them a mechanic. The ordinary dead — the straw-dead, the sick, the old — rest in **Helheim**:
not a place of punishment but a vast, quiet realm of diminished existence. How you died *routed*
you; your funeral rites *equipped* you.

The funeral forms are attested, not invented. Ynglinga saga remembers two ages of the dead: the
**Age of Burning** (cremation, ashes under standing stones) and the **Age of Cairns** (mound burial
with goods, horse, armor) — the dead were *sent off with their tools* [Heim]. This is why the codex
insists the dead *take* their share of the party's wealth: the goods laid with the body are
equipment for the journey, and withholding them is withholding the journey. The darkest routing —
oathbreakers and *níðingar* to **Náströnd**, the corpse-shore — comes from Völuspá's own blackest
image [PE: Völuspá 38], and the codex rightly presents it as ⚠️ priestly warning rather than
verified geography. The goði's certainty about all of this in-world is Volmarr's teaching device:
the table *believes* absolutely, because the culture did.

## II. The Complete Playable Rule

### At a 5E Table

**Death rites are played, not skipped.** When a character dies, the table holds a short funeral —
burning (Age of Burning) or mounding (Age of Cairns), the district's custom or the dead's stated
wish deciding. The procedure, in order:

1. **The laying-out:** the body is prepared — washed, dressed in the best clothes, weapons and tools laid beside it. This takes an hour of game time and is done by kin or companions; skipping it is the first cut against the rites.
2. **The goods:** the dead *take* their share. Record what is laid with them — at minimum their weapon and armor, plus **10% of their personal wealth** (silver, treasure, trade goods) — and remove it from the party's wealth. This is not a tax; it is equipment for the road. A character buried without their tools arrives unarmed, and the goði will say so plainly.
3. **The sending:** burning on a pyre (ashes under a standing stone or scattered on the wind) or mounding (a cairn raised, goods within). Either way, the rite takes half a day and the whole party attends — attendance is part of the rite.
4. **The remembrance goblet:** at the next blót (§IV), a minni-cup is drunk to the dead, with true words spoken over it (§XIX: minni kept grants +1 Hamingja to those who drink truly).

**Skipping the rites** risks the dead walking: *draugr* are not monsters here — they are
**unfuneraled obligations** (§II's *dauðrafriðr*, frith with the dead). If the table skips any step
above, the GM rolls secretly: **1–2 on 1d6**, the dead walks (per §XXV: a wight or ghast stat block,
reskinned — it has *business*, and it cannot be laid until the business is settled *and* the rites
are performed). Even a skipped remembrance goblet leaves the dead restless — dreams of the
unremembered (§XXIII) until the cup is drunk.

**Routing — told by the goði, believed absolutely.** The player does not choose; the *manner of death* routes:

- **Slain in battle, weapon in hand, facing the foe** → the valkyries choose between **Valhöll** and **Fólkvangr**. The choosing is the GM's secret — the table never knows which, and it doesn't matter. *Chosen* matters: a warrior who fell bravely but whose death was witnessed as cowardly (fleeing, begging) is not routed here — the valkyries watch the *manner*, not just the field.
- **Drowned at sea** → **Rán's net**. Feared. A silver offering to Rán before sailing (at least **one ounce of silver**, cast overboard with a spoken word) grants **advantage on one storm-save** during that voyage — one offering, one voyage, one save.
- **Straw-death, sickness, age** → **Helheim's quiet halls**. Not shameful for the old — the elder who dies in bed with kin around them is honored — but a young warrior's dread. A warrior who *chooses* the bed (refusing battle to die quietly) is judged by the culture as cowardice; route them to Helheim, and let the hall's silence say the rest.
- **Oathbreakers and *níðingar*** → **Náströnd**, the corpse-shore. Presented as priestly warning, not verified geography — the goði speaks of it to frighten the living into honor, and the table treats it as the culture treated it: a sermon, not a map. ⚠️
- **Edge cases:** death in holmgang (§XI) counts as battle-death — the duel is lawful combat, weapon in hand. Death by seiðr-curse is routed by *manner*, not cause — a cursed warrior who dies fighting still goes to Valhöll. Death at sea by violence (not drowning) is battle-death if weapon was in hand. Suicide: the culture gave it no honored routing — the goði is evasive, the table understands. Death by draugr or by the dead's business is its own warning: attend to the rites.

**The good death (§III).** A PC who dies in battle, keeping oaths, protecting kin — weapon in hand,
facing the foe — earns a **saga-death**: the table composes a short verse of their deeds (or the GM
delivers one); **half their Orðstír** (rounded down) passes to their named heir or sworn companion
as starting Orðstír (§I); and the *player's next character* begins with **Inspiration and +2
Orðstír** — "born under a bright name." If a drápa was performed at the funeral (§XX), the heir
gains **+1 more** starting Orðstír — the poem is part of the burial goods. The table *celebrates* a
good death the way other tables celebrate a level-up. A PC who dies by cowardice, oathbreaking, or
straw-death (young) leaves nothing: no verse, no inheritance, no bright beginning — and the table
knows it.

### At an OSE Table

- **The funeral rite identical** — laying-out, the dead's share (10% of wealth plus arms, recorded and leaving the party's wealth), burning or mounding, the remembrance goblet at the next blót (+1 Hamingja for those who drink truly, per §XIX).
- **Skipped rites identical:** GM rolls secretly, 1–2 on 1d6 the dead walks — use the OSE **wight or ghoul** entry, reskinned; it has *business* and cannot be laid until the business is settled *and* the rites are performed.
- **Routing identical**, believed absolutely in-world: Valhöll/Fólkvangr for the battle-slain (the valkyries' choosing — the GM's secret); Rán's net for the drowned; Helheim's quiet halls for the straw-dead; Náströnd as priestly warning for oathbreakers. Edge cases (holmgang = battle-death, cursed-but-fighting = Valhöll, the evasive suicide) identical.
- **Rán's offering identical:** one ounce of silver before sailing grants **+2 on one storm-save** (or a re-roll, table's choice) — one offering, one voyage, one save.
- **The good death identical:** saga-verse, half Orðstír to the heir (rounded down), funeral drápa adds +1 — and the player's next character begins with **one free reroll** (Inspiration's OSE form) **and +2 Orðstír**, born under a bright name. The table celebrates it either way.
- Full dual-system reference: `SYSTEM_VARIANTS.md` (Appendix — Codex §XIV).

### Worked Example

A 5E example (illustrative). A PC warrior falls holding a bridge against raiders — weapon in hand,
facing the foe, kin behind her. The table holds the funeral: her sword and byrnie are laid with her,
10% of her silver goes into the mound (recorded; it leaves the party's wealth), and at the next blót
the party drinks the remembrance goblet with true words (+1 Hamingja each, per §XIX). The GM
secretly decides the valkyries' choosing — Valhöll or Fólkvangr, the table never learns which. The
skald performs a drápa at the mound. Her named heir begins with half her Orðstír (rounded down) +1
for the funeral poem; the player's next character begins with Inspiration and +2 Orðstír. Had the
party *skipped* the rites to chase the raiders, the GM would have rolled 1d6 in secret — on a 1 or
2, she walks, with business, until the rites are done. At an OSE table the same: the share leaves
the wealth, the heir's Orðstír is computed identically, and the next character gets the free reroll.

## III. Teaching Beat — What the Table Learns

Death stops being failure and becomes **authorship**: the player writes their character's ending the
way the skalds wrote endings — and learns why a culture that believed this feared the bed more than
the battlefield. The funeral procedure teaches the economy of the dead (the dead *take* their share
— no hoarding against the journey), and the routing teaches the culture's values by geography:
courage goes up to the spear-raftered hall, ordinary life goes quietly down, and dishonor goes to
the corpse-shore the priests warn about. The celebration of the good death rewires the table's
instincts — which is exactly what the belief rewired in the culture.
