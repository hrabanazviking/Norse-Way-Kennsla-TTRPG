## I. Grounding — Why It Is True

In the Viking Age, honor was not a private feeling — it was *public wealth*. The *Hávamál* says it with an axeblow's plainness: *"Cattle die, and kinsmen die, and so one dies one's self; but a noble name will never die, if good renown one gets"* [Háv 76–77]. A man's worth was reckoned in what others said of him, aloud, where all could hear. Secret deeds earned nothing; a gift given in darkness bought no standing — which is why the same poem spends stanzas 40–42 on the economy of giving: *"Gift-givers' friendships are longest found… and gifts with gifts requite"* [Háv 40–42]. Renown was minted by *drengskapr* — the warrior's code of courage, generosity, fairness, and cheerfulness in hardship — and annihilated by its opposite, *níð*: cowardice, treachery, perversion, everything the culture spat at.

What is **Volmarr's homebrew invention** and what is attested history? The culture of reputation-as-currency is thoroughly attested: the sagas are full of men whose good name was their whole capital, of flytings fought because an unanswered insult cost a man his standing, of kings keeping skalds precisely to mint renown into portable poetry. The *numbers* are the invention — the 0–20 scale, all PCs beginning at 5, honor spent one-for-one as a mechanical bonus, half passing to the heir on a good death. No 9th-century goði tracked a sheet marked "Orðstír 12." But the rule is faithful: it models, with dice, the exact economy the sagas describe.

## II. The Complete Playable Rule

### At a 5E Table

**Tracking.** Each PC has **Orðstír 0–20**. All begin at **5** (known in their district, nothing more). The score is **public at the table** — written on a shared sheet or chalked where everyone can see it — because honor exists only in the eyes of others.

**Witness rule *(addition to the codex)*.** A deed counts as "before witnesses" only if at least **one free adult who is not of the PC's own household** saw it and can speak of it. Deeds done before kin alone earn nothing *until* the tale is sung at a gathering (feast, Thing, sumbel) — then it counts, because the hall has witnessed it. Deeds done in total secrecy earn nothing ever: no eyes, no honor. This is the culture's own logic, codified.

**Gain +1** (GM's call, announced aloud so the table witnesses it — the table is the hall):
- Open courage in battle (holding the line, covering a retreat, striking the great blow).
- A generous gift given openly — at least a notable gift (~1 ounce of silver or a thing of worth); lavish hospitality given to guests.
- Wise counsel spoken at the Thing that carries the assembly.
- Keeping an oath at real cost.
- Composing or commissioning a praise-poem for another (§XX).
- Victory in holmgang (§XI).

*(Addition to the codex:)* A single deed cannot earn more than +1. At most **+2 per session** — fame needs time to travel. The GM announces each gain: *"The table witnesses: +1."*

**Loss −1 to −3** (GM's call, announced with the reason):
- Cowardice before witnesses: −2.
- Oathbreaking: −3 (and see §IX's *eiðrofi*).
- Theft by stealth: −2. *(Note: open raiding — víking — is honorable; theft is sneaking.)*
- Insult left unanswered before witnesses: −1.
- Miserliness (withholding hospitality, hoarding): −1.
- Killing a man already beaten who yields: −2.

**Spend — honor is currency, not a score.** Before any Charisma check where your name matters (Persuasion, Intimidation, Performance; gathering warriors, swaying the Thing, hiring ships, being believed), you may **spend Orðstír 1-for-1 as a bonus to the roll**. Spent points return only through new deeds — you cannot hoard reputation; you must keep earning it.
*(Additions to the codex:)* You may spend no more than your current total (you cannot spend into debt), and **no more than +5 on a single roll** — one man's name carries only so far. The spend is declared *before* the roll, aloud: *"I spend 2 of my name."* The table hears it, because the world must hear it.

**Thresholds.**
- **At 0:** *níðingr*-adjacent — no one follows you, no one believes your oath, innkeepers seat you last. (Three níð-marks, §XVII, makes it formal.)
- **At 15+:** saga-famous — strangers know your name, kings seek your counsel, your enemies must answer your insults or lose face themselves *(addition to the codex: enemies who refuse a lawful flyting challenge from a saga-famous PC lose 1 Orðstír before witnesses)*.

**Inheritance.** When a PC dies well (see §XIV, the saga-death), **half their Orðstír, rounded down**, passes to their **named heir or sworn companion** as that character's starting Orðstír. A PC who dies by cowardice, oathbreaking, or straw-death leaves nothing — no inheritance, and the table knows it. *(Addition to the codex:)* The heir must be named aloud at the table before the death, or in the PC's funeral rites (§XIV) — honor cannot be inherited from a corpse nobody claims.

**Interactions.** Orðstír lost to frith-breach (§II), sumbel-vow breaking (§XIX), or ergi-accusations (§XVIII) follows those sections' costs; this section's spend mechanic is the universal one. A níðingr's ( §XVII) Persuasion checks fail automatically where honor is weighed — no amount of spent Orðstír can buy back a ruined name.

### At an OSE Table

Spend **1 Orðstír → +1 on a 2d6 reaction roll** where your name matters, or **−2 on a roll-under CHA check** (1d20 ≤ Charisma made easier). The same caps apply: no more than +5 to a single reaction roll, declared before rolling, and only where the speaker's name is known or is made known ("tell them who I am").

At 0 — níðingr-adjacent: hirelings will not serve and retainers leave. At 15+ — saga-famous: strangers' reaction rolls start one step friendlier *(addition to the codex: treat an initial reaction roll as if one result friendlier than rolled, minimum "friendly")*. Everything else identical — honor is honor in any century.

### Worked Example

Astrid (Orðstír 9) stands at the Thing to plead for her accused brother. She wants the crowd moved. Before rolling, she says aloud: *"I spend 3 of my name"* — dropping to 6 — and rolls Charisma (Persuasion): the die shows 14, +3 from her Charisma and spent honor, and her +2 proficiency, for 19. Had the roll failed, the 3 points would still be gone — spent reputation does not return, only new deeds earn it back. *(Illustrative numbers; a real roll may fall where it falls.)*

## III. Teaching Beat — What the Table Learns

Players discover, through their own resource management, that in this world **reputation is the real hit points**. They start performing generosity and courage *because the mechanics reward being seen doing it* — scheduling their giving for the feast, their courage for the shield-wall, their counsel for the crowded Thing — which is exactly how the culture worked. Nobody lectures them about Viking-Age shame-culture; the spend-and-earn loop *is* the lecture. When a player realizes they must risk their name to use their name — that spending down to 0 is possible and terrifying — they have understood *orðstír* better than any essay could teach.
