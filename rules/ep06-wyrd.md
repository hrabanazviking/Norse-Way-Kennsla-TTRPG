# Wyrd & Ørlög — Full Rule Expansion (The Norse Way, Episode 6)

## I. Grounding — Why It Is True

The Norns are not a metaphor in the sources. In *Völuspá*, at the root of Yggdrasil: *"Thence come the maidens mighty in wisdom, / Three from the dwelling down 'neath the tree; / Urth is one named, Verthandi the next,— / On the wood they scored,— and Skuld the third. / Laws they made there, and life allotted / To the sons of men, and set their fates"* [PE: Völuspá 20]. Urðr (what has become), Verðandi (what is becoming), Skuld (what shall be) carve *ørlög* — literally "primal law" — laid down at birth. Snorri's Prose Edda keeps the same doctrine: the Norns come to every child born and shape its life [SnE]. Fate is not a script you read; it is a weave you are caught in.

The heroic response is not escape but *meeting*: *"One thing I know that never dies, / The fame of a dead man's deeds"* [Háv 78], and before it: *"Cattle die, and kinsmen die, / And so one dies one's self; / But a noble name will never die, / If good renown one gets"* [Háv 76–77]. Straw-death (dying in bed) was the dread; a good death was the crown. Óðinn himself bought his wisdom by paying a price — *"offered I was / To Othin, myself to myself"* [Háv 139] — which is why wyrd-debt can only be cleared by paying in kind: the cosmos keeps accounts, and the currency is deeds.

⚠️ *What the sources do not give us:* no saga describes a counted "wyrd-debt" or a formal Norn-reading rite at a set threshold. The Norse felt fate as atmosphere, not arithmetic. The counting is Volmarr's game machinery — honest homebrew built on an attested worldview, and labeled as such wherever it appears below.

**What is homebrew here:** the Norns, ørlög, and the honor-in-death ethic are attested. The *mechanics* — the birth rune drawn at creation, Wyrd-debt as a counted stat, the Norn's reading at 5 debt, the saga-death inheritance formula — are Volmarr's invention, built to make fate playable at the table.

## II. The Complete Playable Rule

### At a 5E Table

**The birth rune.** At character creation, the GM draws the PC's birth rune from the Elder Futhark (d24 on the table below, or draw from `runes.json`). It is an *omen, not a stat*: a flavor-truth about the PC's wyrd. The GM may invoke it **once per session**: either **advantage on one fitting roll** (the roll must connect to the rune's meaning — GM's call), or a **whispered warning** — a short image or line delivered privately, true but never a plan. The player may *refuse* the warning; refusing a true warning gains 1 Wyrd-debt (expansion: the Norns do not like being ignored).

**Reading the birth rune at the table** (expansion — GM guidance):
- Reveal the rune's *omen* at creation, not a mechanical effect. The player should feel named, not buffed.
- The once-per-session invocation is the GM's tool, not the player's: the GM decides when the rune's meaning fits the roll.
- The whispered warning should be *imagery*, never instruction: "you dream of a thorn hedge across a road you have not yet taken" — not "don't go to Birka."
- A fitting use is generous: if the player argues the connection well, allow it. The Norns reward those who read the signs.
- Never invoke the rune to *punish* — it is an omen of the PC's wyrd, not a leash.

**Re-drawing** (expansion): the birth rune is drawn once, at creation, and never re-drawn — it is the ørlög laid down at birth, not a mood. If the GM's physical draw feels wrong for the character, re-draw *before the first session only*, openly. After play begins, the rune stands.

**Wyrd-debt.** A silent counter, starting at 0, tracked privately by the GM (the players know their own total only if a seeress tells them — expansion). Accrued from:
- **Frith-breach:** 1 per breach (§II).
- **Oathbreaking:** 3 for *eiðrofi* (§IX); 1 for a lesser broken vow such as a failed braga-goblet boast (expansion — the Norns heard it too).
- **Coercive magic:** per §V (the caster's failed Wisdom save).
- **Refusing a Norn-whispered warning:** 1 (expansion).
- **Mocking a true dream or spá:** 1 (expansion — contempt for the gods' postal service, §XXIII–XXIV).

It is **invisible to all but seeresses** — a völva, seið-worker, or wise one may perceive another's Wyrd-debt with a Wisdom (Insight) check, DC 15 (expansion). Reading *your own* debt requires a spá or útiseta (§XXIV): the rite names the price, as rites do.

The GM may **spend** a PC's Wyrd-debt 1-for-1 to impose **disadvantage on one roll**, describing it as the world turning cold: a rope frays, a horse shies, a friend mishears (§II). Spent debt stays spent — it is a price, not a loan. The GM should spend openly (*"your wyrd-debt turns against you here"*) so the table learns the cause-and-effect, not secretly.

**What the debt feels like** (expansion — atmosphere the GM may use, never mechanics the players can game):

| Debt | The world around the debtor |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Nothing any but a völva would notice. Perhaps the debtor's shadow seems a half-step slow. |
| 3–4 | Animals go quiet around them. Milk sours a day early. Small things go missing and turn up broken. |
| 5+ | The Norn's reading has been spoken — the air before a storm. Children stare. Old folk make the warding sign. |

**Debt and the kin-line** (expansion): Wyrd-debt is personal — it does not pass to heirs. But a PC who dies with debt *unmet and unconfessed* leaves a stain the table remembers: their heir begins with the birth rune **Hagalaz** (hail from a clear sky) at the GM's discretion. The Norns keep long books.

**The Norn's reading.** At **5 Wyrd-debt**, the GM performs a reading, openly at the table: **three runes drawn** and interpreted as a **doom the PC cannot dodge — only meet well**. Example: *"Thurisaz — the thorn. Before winter's end you will face a foe you cannot defeat. How you face him is yours."* The doom is binding in *price*, never in *choice*: the GM may never use it to force a decision, only to name what the decision will cost. The doom must be *specific enough to recognize, open enough to meet freely* — name the shape (a foe, a storm, a choice between kin and oath), never the move.

- **Fleeing the doom** counts as cowardice before witnesses (−1 Orðstír, §I), and the debt remains. The Norns are patient.
- **Meeting it** — facing it openly, whatever the outcome — clears **all** Wyrd-debt. If the doom is met and survived with witnesses watching: **+2 Orðstír** (a deed of saga-weight, expansion). If the PC dies to it facing the foe: it counts as a **saga-death** automatically, no further test.
- **A second reading** (expansion): if debt climbs to 5 again after a reading was met, the Norns read again — but the second doom is *heavier*: the GM draws three runes and the doom now names a *kin-member or sworn companion* as part of its price. Fate escalates with those who will not learn.

**The reading, spoken aloud** (expansion — example wordings; the GM should speak these slowly, laying the runes as they speak):
- *"Ansuz, Nauthiz, Hagalaz. A word you speak this winter will bind you. Need will teach you what the word cost. And hail comes from a clear sky — the ruin will not be your fault, but the word will be."*
- *"Tiwaz, Isa, Perthro. Justice frozen: a judgment you must give, and give rightly, though it cost you. The cup is shaken — chance will have its say in how it lands."*
- *"Eihwaz, Laguz, Othala. The narrow place is water: you will cross it, or your kin will lose what your fathers kept. Endure. Water finds its way; so must you."*
The pattern: two runes name the *shape*, the third names the *price*. Never name the choice.

**Clearing debt otherwise:** a great public deed (GM's call, witnessed) or a blót of real cost (a *great* offering or better, §IV) clears all Wyrd-debt. Private atonement clears nothing — the Norns are not fooled by secrecy. **Spá and útiseta do not clear debt** — they only *name* it (§XXIV); knowing the shape of doom binds you tighter (+1 Wyrd-debt for the asking, as the codex already rules).

**Saga-death.** A PC who dies **in battle, keeping oaths, protecting kin — weapon in hand, facing the foe** — earns a saga-death:
1. The table composes, or the GM delivers, a **short verse** (at least four lines) of their deeds. The verse should be *speakable* — kennings encouraged, plain words allowed.
2. **Half their Orðstír (rounded down)** passes to their **named heir or sworn companion** as starting Orðstír (§I).
3. The player's **next character begins with Inspiration and +2 Orðstír**, "born under a bright name."

The heir must have been **named before death** — in a will spoken at sumbel, in a witnessed declaration, or in the death scene's last words (expansion). If no heir was named, the inheritance goes to the PC's *sworn companion* of longest standing (GM's call); if none exists, the verse is still spoken, but nothing passes — the line ends, and the table feels it. A PC who dies by cowardice, oathbreaking, or straw-death leaves nothing: no verse, no inheritance, and the table knows it.

**Dying well vs. dying usefully** (expansion): a saga-death requires *how*, not *why*. Dying to hold a door so kin escape qualifies; dying in a duel you provoked for vanity does not — the GM judges the *manner* (oaths kept, kin protected, foe faced), not the cause's wisdom. A glorious fool's death is still a saga-death if he died facing the foe with his oaths intact.

### Quick Reference — Wyrd at a Glance

- **Birth rune:** drawn once at creation; GM invokes 1/session (advantage on a fitting roll, or a whispered warning).
- **Debt sources:** frith-breach +1 · failed boast +1 · mocked dream/spá +1 · refused warning +1 · coercive magic (per §V) · *eiðrofi* +3.
- **GM spends debt:** 1 point → disadvantage on one roll (5E) / +2 harder on roll-under (OSE), spent openly.
- **At 5 debt:** the Norn's reading — three runes, a doom to meet, never a choice to force.
- **Clearing:** meet the doom · great public deed · costly blót (great+ offering, petition names the clearing).
- **Saga-death:** verse spoken · half Orðstír to the *named* heir · next character Inspiration + +2 Orðstír (OSE: one free reroll).

### At an OSE Table

- **Birth-rune invocation:** **−2 on a roll-under check** (instead of advantage) on one fitting roll per session, or the whispered warning (identical). Refusing a true warning still gains 1 Wyrd-debt.
- **Perceiving debt:** a völva or wise one rolls **1d20 ≤ Wisdom − 2** to sense another's Wyrd-debt.
- **Spending debt:** the GM spends 1 Wyrd-debt for **+2 on a roll-under check** (harder) against the PC — the world turning cold either system.
- **Saga-death:** your next character begins with **one free reroll** (instead of Inspiration) and +2 Orðstír. The verse, the half-inheritance, the heir-naming rule, and the second-reading escalation are identical.
- **Everything else is identical** — wyrd is wyrd in any century.

### Worked Example

*Illustrative only — hypothetical rolls.* Brandr the fighter (Wyrd-debt 4) breaks hearth-frith in a tavern brawl, striking a man under a host's roof. That is his fifth debt point. The GM lays three runes on the table: *Hagalaz, Nauthiz, Tiwaz.* "Hail from a clear sky," the GM says. "Before the sea opens in spring, a ruin you did not earn will find you — and a hard choice will follow it. Justice will cost you."

In the next session, Brandr's player asks for advantage on a Persuasion roll to talk down a jarl; the GM spends 1 Wyrd-debt and imposes disadvantage instead — *"your wyrd-debt turns against you here"* — describing the jarl's hound growling at nothing behind Brandr. The roll fails. The table has learned the cause-and-effect.

In spring, a rival burns Brandr's boathouse. Brandr could flee to the hills (cowardice: −1 Orðstír, debt stays) — instead he walks into the Thing, names the arsonist, and demands holmgang (§XI). Win or lose, the doom is *met*: all debt cleared, and if he falls facing the foe with his oaths intact, the table speaks his verse and his heir takes half his honor. Wyrd named the price. Brandr chose how to pay it.

## III. Teaching Beat — What the Table Learns

Players stop fearing character death and start fearing *meaningless* death — the exact inversion of modern play instincts. The birth rune teaches them that the world has opinions about them from the first session; the Norn's reading teaches that a doom *named* is a doom that can be *met* — which is the entire Norse heroic ethic compressed into one mechanic; and the saga-death teaches the oldest lesson of the North: what dies is the body, what lives is the name — but only if the table watched you earn it. The GM's discipline — naming prices, never forcing choices — is the craft lesson underneath: fate as a teacher, not a railroad.
