# Feud & Revenge — Full Rule Expansion (The Norse Way, Episode 20)

## I. Grounding — Why It Is True

The feud is the saga's engine and the society's wound. *Njáls saga* is, at its heart, a manual of
how killings compound: a death demands weregild or blood; blood demands blood; each settlement that
fails raises the price — until the burning of Bergþórshvoll, where the whole ledger goes up in fire
[Njál]. Vengeance was not rage — it was **obligation**. A kinsman who did not pursue a killing was
himself shamed; the debt was owed to the kin-group, and the kin-group *was* the person. This is why
the codex records the debt against the kin-group rather than the killer: in this world, the family
was the legal unit.

But the system was not mere savagery — it *worked*, until it didn't. A feud settled honorably
(weregild paid in full, at the Thing, before witnesses) ended with handshakes and sometimes
marriage; the law's machinery of weregild, oath-helpers, and public settlement was a genuine peace
technology [Njál]. The codex's temperature ladder — Cool, Warm, Burning — is Volmarr's mechanical
translation of that observed arc: debts that cool when honored and burn when neglected. The hidden
knife's níð-mark is the culture's own verdict, not the game's: the sagas despise the secret killing
precisely because it evades the ledger — no announcement, no witnesses, no law.

## II. The Complete Playable Rule

### At a 5E Table

**The debt.** Every **unlawful killing** — outside holmgang (§XI), open battle, or lawful execution
(Thing-sentenced, §VII) — creates a **blood-debt** against the killer's **kin-group**, owed to the
victim's **kin-group**. The kin-group = blood kin, foster-kin (§XXVI), and sworn household — the
people whose honor rises and falls with yours. Record every debt in the open ledger: ***who* (the
killer, named), *of whom* (the victim, named), *when* (the season), *witnessed by whom*.** The debt
has a **price** — the weregild ladder (§VI, `weregild.json`) — and a **temperature**:

- ***Cool* (negotiable):** the killing is known; the victim's kin have named their price or signaled willingness to talk. Messengers can travel between the groups.
- ***Warm* (insults exchanged, Thing demanded):** words have been said in public; one side or both have demanded the Thing take it up. Hospitality between the groups is withdrawn (§VIII). Travelers go armed.
- ***Burning* (raids, counter-killings, no talk):** messengers are turned away or killed; the groups strike at each other's people, farms, and cattle. No negotiation is possible until the temperature falls.
- ***Settled*:** weregild paid in full at the Thing, publicly. The debt is closed — it cannot be reopened for the same killing.

**Temperature rises** one step per month the debt goes unaddressed (no messenger sent, no Thing
demanded, no payment offered), or **immediately** upon any new insult, theft, or violence between
the groups. **Temperature falls** one step when both sides attend the Thing peacefully and speak to
the debt (even without settling — the forms of law cool the blood), or when a weregild payment is
made in proper public form (§VII).

**Edge cases on what creates a debt:**

- Killing in **holmgang, open battle, or lawful execution**: no debt. The law has already spoken.
- Killing a **thrall**: a debt of *property*, not blood — owed to the thrall's owner at the thrall's price (§VI), and it does not carry temperature; it is a fine, not a feud.
- Killing **by accident** (a hunting mishap, a shipboard mischance): the debt exists, but begins at *Cool* and the killer's open admission *and* immediate offer of weregild can settle it before it warms — the culture distinguished mischance from murder, but the dead still have kin.
- **Killing the wrong man** in vengeance (striking one who holds no share of the debt): a *new* blood-debt, recorded separately, against the avenger's kin-group. The ledger does not forgive arithmetic errors.
- **Deaths at sea** between feuding groups: if witnessed as deliberate, a debt; if the sea took them, the sea's business — the goði will say the valkyries and Rán sort their own (§XIV).

**Feud-turns.** At *Burning*, the GM runs **feud-turns**: each session, roll **1d6** — on **4+**,
the feud acts. The players may be the debtors, the creditors, or caught between. What the feud does
(roll 1d6, or the GM chooses what the story demands):

- **1–2:** *Theft and spite* — cattle driven off, a storehouse emptied, a boat's lines cut. Wealth bleeds; no blood yet.
- **3–4:** *Fire and wounding* — a farm burned, a kinsman left bloodied at the door as a message. A wounded kinsman is an insult that warms everything it touches.
- **5–6:** *Ambush* — a kinsman is slain. This creates a **new blood-debt** in the other direction — the ledger grows, exactly as the sagas show.

Each feud-turn at *Burning* also risks escalation beyond the two groups: neighbors take sides, the
Thing is petitioned, and the district's frith (§II) begins to fray.

**Settlement (§VII).** Weregild **paid in full at the Thing, publicly** — the silver weighed before
witnesses, the forms spoken — cools the feud to *Settled*. Both sides gain **+1 Orðstír** (honorable
closure is honorable). **Underpayment, late payment, or payment without the proper public forms**
keeps it *Warm* — the debt is acknowledged but not closed, and the temperature will rise again. A
settlement may include marriage-alliance, fosterage (§XXVI), or other bonds — the sagas' handshakes
— at the table's invention; the mechanics require only the full weregild and the public forms.

**Vengeance taken** (blood for blood, done **openly, announced** — the avenger declares the killing
before witnesses, naming the debt it answers): the debt is **transferred, not erased** — the
killer's kin now hold the claim, and the ledger turns its page. Open, announced vengeance costs **no
níð-mark** (it is lawful in the culture's eyes) but grants **no Orðstír** either; it is duty, not
glory. **Secret vengeance** (murder by night, denial after) = **1 níð-mark + the debt stands** — the
culture despised the hidden knife, and the ledger does not recognize what the law cannot see.
Announced vengeance against one who holds *no share* of the debt is murder, not vengeance — a new
debt, as above.

**Letting it fester.** Any PC whose kin-group holds an **unsettled blood-debt at *Burning* for three
months** loses **1 Hamingja per month** — the feud poisons the luck-line (§XVI), as the sagas show
again and again. The loss continues until the debt cools below Burning or is settled. A PC may
*leave* the kin-group's feud only by formal renunciation before the Thing — which costs 2 Orðstír
and the kin's regard, because the culture understood exactly what desertion was.

### At an OSE Table

- **Recording the debt** — who, of whom, when, witnessed by whom — is **identical**; the ledger is system-neutral. The kin-group (blood, foster-kin, sworn household) is identical.
- **Temperature and its rises are identical:** Cool → Warm → Burning, one step per unaddressed month or on any new insult, theft, or violence; one step down for peaceful Thing attendance or properly formed payment.
- **Edge cases identical:** holmgang/battle/lawful execution = no debt; thrall-killing = property fine; accidents begin Cool; killing the wrong man = a new debt; deaths at sea at the goði's discretion.
- **Feud-turns identical:** at Burning, each session roll **1d6** — on 4+ the feud acts; the 1d6 severity table (theft / fire and wounding / ambush) is **identical** — OSE morale and hireling rules make the fallout bite, but the trigger is the same die. A slain kinsman creates a new blood-debt in the other direction, either system.
- **Settlement identical:** weregild paid in full at the Thing, publicly, cools to Settled, both sides +1 Orðstír — weregild paid in **gp** from the `weregild.json` ladder; underpaid, late, or improper payment stays Warm.
- **Vengeance identical:** open, announced vengeance *transfers* the debt (no níð-mark, no Orðstír); secret vengeance = 1 níð-mark + the debt stands. Renunciation before the Thing costs 2 Orðstír, either system.
- **Festering identical:** blood-debt at Burning for three months → the PC loses **1 Hamingja per month** until it cools or settles.
- Full dual-system reference: `SYSTEM_VARIANTS.md` (Appendix — Codex §XXI).

### Worked Example

A 5E example (illustrative). At a feast, a drunken PC strikes down a man of a neighboring kin-group
over an insult — no holmgang, no battle, no law: an unlawful killing. The GM records the blood-debt
openly: *who* (the PC, named), *of whom* (the slain man, named), *when* (this winter), *witnessed by
whom* (the whole hall). The debt begins at *Cool*, priced from the weregild ladder. The PC's kin
send no messenger and offer nothing for a month: the debt warms. At the spring Thing, insults are
exchanged publicly and the victim's kin demand judgment: still *Warm*, but now the forms are
engaged. Then a hothead of the victim's kin burns the PC's family's boathouse — new violence between
the groups: the debt goes *Burning*, immediately. Each session now, the GM rolls 1d6 for the
feud-turn: on a 4+, the feud acts — say a 5, an ambush, and a kinsman of the PC's group is slain.
That is a *new* blood-debt, now owed the other way. The wise move was always the same: weregild, in
full, at the Thing, before witnesses — Settled, +1 Orðstír both sides, handshakes. At an OSE table
every die and every step is identical; only the currency of the weregild (gp) needs no translation.

## III. Teaching Beat — What the Table Learns

The feud turns the party's violence into **politics with a ledger**. Players learn to ask, before
the sword comes out, *"who will own this killing tomorrow?"* — which is the single most Viking
question in the codex. They learn that vengeance was obligation rather than rage (no glory in it, no
Orðstír — only duty done or duty shirked), that the hidden knife was despised precisely because it
cheated the law, and that the system *could* end feuds — the handshake and the weregild were real —
which makes the Burning of Bergþórshvoll land as tragedy rather than inevitability. The festering
rule teaches the saga's deepest pattern: the feud doesn't just kill men, it *poisons luck*, and the
whole kin-group pays.
