# The Norse Way — Episode 19: Skaldcraft (Poems That Mint Honor)

**Video:** `videos/ep19-skaldcraft.mp4` · 1080×1920 · ~30s · Narration: Unnr (Rumi voice)

## Transcript

> The skald was not an entertainer — he was a mint. A drápa, the praise-poem with its refrain, made a chieftain's deeds permanent and portable; a king without a skald would be forgotten. The reverse cut too: níðvísur, scorn-verses, could flay a reputation alive. Condemned, Egill Skalla-Grímsson saved his own head in one night with Höfuðlausn — Head-Ransom. At this table, poems are legal tender: perform them, sell them, and never insult a skald — the weapon cuts both ways.

## The Rule (from HOMEBREW_CODEX.md §XX)

- **Composing** (downtime, or the night before the Thing): the player declares a subject and a kind — *drápa* (praise) or *níðvísa* (scorn) — and makes a **Charisma (Performance)** check, **DC 15**. The player must speak **at least a full stanza aloud at the table** (four lines minimum; kennings encouraged — the GM keeps a kenning-list as reward-bait). Success mints the poem as an **item**: *"Drápa for Jarl X, by [skald]"* — a trade good of the renown economy.
- **Drápa effects:** the subject gains **+1 Orðstír** among all who hear the poem performed (the skald chooses where to perform it — halls, Things, feasts). The skald gains **+1 Orðstír per notable patron praised (max +3 per poem)** — patrons *pay* for this, in silver, gifts, or protection, and the GM prices a commissioned drápa at **no less than a good sword**. A drápa performed for a *fallen* warrior at his funeral (§XIV) grants his **heir +1 starting Orðstír** — the poem is part of the burial goods.
- **Níðvísa effects:** the subject loses **1 Orðstír** among hearers and must answer — by flyting (§X), holmgang (§XI), or a counter-poem — or take a **níð-mark** for letting scorn stand (§XVII). A níðvísa composed *falsely* (the skald knows the charge is untrue) earns the *skald* **1 níð-mark**: the weapon cuts both ways.
- **Head-ransom rule:** a condemned PC (or NPC the party champions) may, as Egill did, compose through the night before execution. One Performance check at **disadvantage** (no sleep, terror, dawn coming), **DC 20**. Success: the judge *must* hear the poem, and any ruler of honor is bound by custom to **spare the skald** who has so honored him — the GM plays this straight. This is the codex's **one built-in miracle**, because the saga records it.

## Teaching Beat

Players learn why Viking kings kept poets the way modern states keep mints — and why insulting a skald was dangerous. The party will start *commissioning* poems about themselves, which means they start caring about being worth a poem about. The economy of praise does the teaching.

## At an OSE Table

- Composing is **1d20 ≤ Charisma** (DC 15 in 5E terms); the player still speaks the full stanza aloud at the table — kennings encouraged, the kenning-list still reward-bait.
- A successful poem becomes the item *"Drápa for Jarl X, by [skald]"* — **identical**; commissioned drápur are priced in gp, no less than a good sword.
- Drápa effects **identical**: subject +1 Orðstír among hearers; skald +1 Orðstír per notable patron (max +3); funeral drápa grants the heir +1 starting Orðstír.
- Níðvísa effects **identical**: subject −1 Orðstír among hearers, answer or take a níð-mark; a false níðvísa earns the *skald* the níð-mark.
- Head-ransom: compose through the night, one check at **disadvantage** (roll twice, keep the worse) against a hard target — success binds the honorable judge to spare the skald, played straight, exactly as codex.
