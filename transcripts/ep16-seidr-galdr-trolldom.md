## Transcript

> The Norse knew three magics, and feared each differently. Seiðr — the witch's art of prophecy and fate-weaving, ruled by Freyja; shameful for men, who risked the ergi-taint for practicing it. Galdr — rune-chanting, the respectable magic: carved and sung, protection and healing, practiced openly even by goðar. Trolldom — the hearth-magic of wards and workings, small and domestic. ## The Rule (from HOMEBREW_CODEX.md §V)

- The three arts are distinct: **Seiðr** — prophecy, fate-weaving, spirit-speaking, weather-working, Freyja's art, practiced in trance with chanting (*seiðlæti*) and a high seat; for *men* it carried the **ergi** taint — Loki's taunt to Óðinn stands in the record, and King Haraldr burned his own son Ragnvald with eighty warlocks, "which work was much praised." **Galdr** — rune-chanting, the *respectable* magic: protection, healing, binding, victory, each rune carved *and sung*, magic-as-*price-paid* after Óðinn's sacrifice on the tree. **Trolldom** — hearth-and-field folk magic: purification (*hreinsa*), wards (*hlífðarverk*), road-opening (*vegaopnun*), prosperity charms, drawn from Volmarr's own trolldom chart.
- **Shared law — Intent → Ritual → Incantation.** No silent, instant casting: magic takes *time* (minutes to hours), *materials* (carved staves, herbs, an offering), and *witnesses or solitude deliberately chosen*.
- **The taboo against coercion is absolute:** all magic accrues Wyrd-debt (§III) when it coerces — overriding another's will risks backlash from their *fylgja* and the gods. A coercive working forces a **Wisdom save by the caster**; on failure the working **rebounds** and the GM describes the fylgja's price.
- **Seiðr** is not a class feature — it is *learned* from a practitioner at great cost. Trance requires a high seat, chanting assistants, and an hour. A male practitioner risks the ergi-taint: each public working costs **−1 Orðstír** (−3 if mocked in flyting after) unless the community already honors him as *seiðmaðr* — rare, and itself saga-worthy. A *völva* or *seiðkona* gains **+Orðstír for true prophecies** — but NPCs keep their distance; hospitality is given warily.
- **Galdr** is this world's "wizardry," deliberately *not* D&D wizardry: each practitioner knows a small set of **rune-songs** (start with 3, learn more only from teachers or grimoires). Each song = one rune + one narrow effect — *Týr* for victory in lawful combat, *Bjarkan* for healing, *Naud* for enduring hardship. Casting = carving the rune (a material focus) + chanting aloud (1 minute) + a Charisma or Wisdom check. Effects are **subtle and bounded**: no fireballs — a wound knits, a storm hesitates, a foe's courage falters. Socially respectable; goðar practice it openly.
- **Trolldom** anyone may learn: small workings, small costs — a purification smoking, a ward-stave over the door, a prosperity bowl. Effects are *minor and domestic* (advantage on one save, a night of untroubled sleep, milk that doesn't sour). Cost is material and time, not spiritual — but a trolldom-worker who meddles in seiðr's business (curses, love-coercion) is judged as a seiðr-worker.

## Teaching Beat

Players learn that magic had a *social price tag* and a *gendered shape* — and that the most powerful magic in the Norse world was the kind a wise man was *ashamed* to practice. The ergi mechanic teaches more about Norse masculinity in one session than any lecture.

## At an OSE Table

- Intent → Ritual → Incantation is **identical** — magic takes time, materials, and witnesses in any system.
- Coercion: the caster makes a **Save vs Spells**; on failure the working rebounds — the fylgja's price, described by the GM.
- Seiðr effects and the ergi-taint costs (−1 Orðstír per public working, −3 if flyting-mocked) are **identical**; the trance still needs a high seat, assistants, and an hour.
- Galdr: 3 rune-songs at start; casting = carve + chant aloud (1 minute) + **1d20 ≤ Charisma or Wisdom**; effects subtle and bounded, exactly as codex.
- Trolldom: **identical** — minor domestic effects (e.g. +2 on one save, one untroubled night), paid in materials and time.
