## The Rule (from HOMEBREW_CODEX.md §XXVI)

- Blood was law, but **fosterage** was love made legal: Njáll fostered Hǫskuldr Þráinsson, and the love between them shaped the whole tragedy that followed [Njál]. A child raised in another's hall belonged to *both* houses — and both houses owed the child. Foster-brothers (*fóstbræðr*) swore oaths that outweighed convenience, and the sagas judge harshly those who betray them.
- **Foster-ties** (declared at character creation or forged in play): each PC may name one **foster-father/mother** or **foster-sibling** (NPC or fellow PC). The tie is *mechanical*:
  - Spend **Orðstír on behalf of** your foster-kin (their Persuasion checks, their Thing cases) — and they may spend for you. Honor is pooled between the fostered, because the world sees you as one house.
  - **Hamingja bequest** (§XVI) flows to foster-kin as freely as to blood.
  - **Betraying** foster-kin (abandoning them in battle, testifying against them falsely, refusing them shelter): **2 níð-marks** — worse than betraying blood, because fosterage was *chosen*.
- **Kin-claims** (the reward/punish web): at the start of each adventure, the GM may present a **kin-claim** — a kinsman's feud, debt, wedding, or lawsuit demanding the PC's presence or silver. Answering it: **+1 Orðstír** (the kin-group's standing rises, and yours with it) and the kin-group *owes you* (a future favor, banked). Ignoring it without lawful excuse: **−1 Orðstír** and **−1 Hamingja** — the kin-group's luck withdraws from the ungrateful, and the table hears about it.
- **Oath-helpers** (§VII): at the Thing, the number of oath-helpers you can field equals your standing kin + foster-kin + sworn companions *willing to stand*. Every ignored kin-claim shrinks that number. The mechanic makes visible what the sagas assume: *your power is your people*.

## Teaching Beat

The lone-wolf PC discovers they are *mechanically* weaker than the entangled one. Players learn to cultivate their NPC relationships the way other games cultivate magic items — because here, people *are* the magic items.
