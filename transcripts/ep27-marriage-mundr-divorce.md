## The Rule (from HOMEBREW_CODEX.md §XXVII)

- Marriage was *treaty*. The **mundr** — the bride-price paid by the groom's kin to the bride's guardian — sealed the alliance, and the bride's **heimanfylgja** (dowry) traveled with her as her own security.
- **Arranging a marriage** (a downtime endeavor, or the campaign's center): the two kin-groups negotiate **mundr** (use the weregild ladder, `weregild.json`, as the price-scale — a high-status bride commands a chieftain's weregild and more). Negotiation is a **Charisma (Persuasion)** contest between the families' spokesmen, best of three, with Orðstír spendable (§I) — because this is *reputation* negotiating.
- **The alliance:** a sealed marriage creates a **treaty** between the kin-groups: mutual oath-helper rights (§XXVI), shared Hamingja bequest eligibility, and a standing **+1 Orðstír** for both houses while the marriage stands and is fruitful. Marrying *up* (into a luck-bright line, §XVI): the lower house gains **+1 Hamingja** — men married into luck deliberately, and said so.
- **Divorce** was real, legal, and accessible: in *Njáls saga*, Unnr Marðardóttir divorces Hrútr Herjólfsson by declaring herself divorced before witnesses, on lawful grounds, and the Thing upholds it [Njál]. Declared before witnesses, with lawful grounds — violence, impotence unremedied, the famous "cross-dressing" clauses (later law-codes; the *principle* of accessible divorce is saga-solid): the marriage-treaty ends; mundr is *returned or forfeit* depending on fault (GM judges; the Thing may be asked, §VII). The divorced woman keeps her heimanfylgja — her economic independence is *the point* of the dowry system.
- **Marriage as feud-tool:** marrying across a blood-debt (§XXI) is the classic settlement — a *Settled* feud sealed by marriage grants both houses **+2 Orðstír** instead of +1. The wedding that ends the war is the saga's favorite ending, and now it pays.

## Teaching Beat

Romance becomes *statecraft*, and players feel the difference: a wedding here is not a love scene but a treaty signing with a feast attached — and a divorce is not a failure but a legal instrument. The modern player's instincts misfire productively.
