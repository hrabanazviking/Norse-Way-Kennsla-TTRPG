## The Rule (from HOMEBREW_CODEX.md §XXIII)

- The sagas treat dreams as *dispatches*, not metaphors. Dream after dream in the sagas warns of death, betrayal, and doom — and the wise *listen*; the doomed do not. To ignore a true dream was not skepticism. It was *negligence*, and the sagas bill it accordingly.
- **The dream-roll:** at each long rest taken in a place of significance (before battle, after a killing, at a holy site, during Yule), the GM rolls secretly: on **1–4 on 1d6**, the sleeper dreams *true*. The GM gives the dream as imagery — never as instruction.
- **Interpretation:** any character may attempt to read the dream — **Wisdom (Insight)** DC 13, advantage if a völva, goði, or recognized wise one does the reading (§XXIV). Success: the GM states the warning plainly (*"Do not take the mountain road," "Your oath-helper means you harm"*). Failure: the reading is muddled — the GM gives two meanings, one true.
- **Heeding:** acting on a rightly-read dream (changing the plan, posting a guard, refusing the voyage) grants **+1 Hamingja** (§XVI) — the universe rewards those who listen. The warned-against disaster, if it was coming, is *softened*: the GM reduces its force (fewer ambushers, the storm glances off, the traitor's knife finds mail).
- **Ignoring:** a rightly-read dream ignored = **−1 Hamingja** and the disaster arrives at *full* force. A dream *mocked* ("dreams are wind") = **−1 Orðstír** among all who heard the mockery — contempt for the dream-world is contempt for the gods' postal service.
- **Dream-debt:** if a PC dies having ignored a true dream that warned of that very death, the saga records it — the survivors' retelling grants the dead no inherited Orðstír bonus (§I). The culture's epitaph for the dream-deaf: *he was warned*.

## Teaching Beat

Dreams become the party's early-warning radar — but one they must *interpret*, never one that hands them answers. Players learn the saga-mind: the world is always speaking; wisdom is the discipline of listening.
