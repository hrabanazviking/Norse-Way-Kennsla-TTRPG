## The Rule (from HOMEBREW_CODEX.md §XI)

- **Challenge & stakes:** the challenger names the grievance and the stakes (weregild, land, a public apology) before witnesses. The challenged may decline only at −3 Orðstír — unless the challenge is *níð*-born, when declining is social death.
- **The ground:** a ring marked with stones; seconds and witnesses stand outside. (The hazel-pole detail and the three-shield custom are saga-tradition — historically uncertain; the GM may simplify.)
- **The fight:** run as normal combat, with two ritual constraints: (1) no ally may interfere — interference is a grave frith-breach and voids the duel; (2) the duel ends when the agreed condition is met — *first blood*, *yielding*, or *death* — as sworn beforehand.
- **Aftermath:** the victor gains +2 Orðstír for a just duel; the slain is owed no weregild *if the duel was lawful*. An unlawful killing in the ring is murder, and the Thing will hear it.

## Teaching Beat

Players learn that even killing had *liturgy* — and that the duel existed so that feuds could end *without* burning the district down. Violence, fenced by ritual, was a peacemaking tool.
