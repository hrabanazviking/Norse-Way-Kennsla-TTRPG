## Transcript

> Outlawry was not freedom. It was the edge of the world. Lesser outlawry: three years banished, property forfeit, minus three honor until return, luck capped at five. Full outlawry, forest-walking: no frith protects you, anyone may kill you without weregild, none may shelter you. You live by hunting; each week without shelter brings a Constitution save against exhaustion, worse in winter. The sentence lifts only by royal pardon, a worse outlaw's death, or a deed the Thing votes home.

## The Rule (from HOMEBREW_CODEX.md §XXII)

- The law had two exiles. **Fjörbaugsgarðr** — "lesser outlawry" — meant three years' banishment: property confiscated, but life protected if the exile stayed away. **Skóggangr** — "forest-walking," full outlawry — meant the man was *outside the law entirely*: any man might kill him without penalty, none might feed or shelter him on pain of sharing his fate.
- **Lesser outlawry** (sentence at the Thing, §VII): the PC must leave the district for three years. Property is forfeit to the injured party. While exiled: **−3 Orðstír** (recoverable on lawful return), Hamingja **capped at 5** — the world is narrower now. Shelter from kin is permitted *once* per year without penalty to the shelterer; more, and the shelterer answers at the Thing.
- **Full outlawry (skóggangr):** the sentence is read, and the PC becomes *óbotamaðr* — a man with no price, because no price can buy him back.
  - **No frith** (§II): none of the eight circles protect you. Anyone may kill you without weregild; no Thing will hear your case.
  - **No shelter:** any NPC who feeds or houses you *knowingly* risks the same sentence. The GM should make every door a decision.
  - **Survival play:** the outlaw lives by hunting, fishing, and the charity of the brave few. Track **Exposure**: each week in the wilds without proper shelter, **Constitution save DC 12 + 1 per consecutive week**, or gain one level of **exhaustion**. Winter doubles the DC — Grettir's saga is a winter saga for a reason.
  - **The price on your head:** the GM sets a silver bounty. Bounty-hunters are now a wandering encounter table — and *killing* a hunter in fair fight earns no new debt (you are outside the law; the law cannot count your kills), but it earns **+1 Orðstír among outlaws, thieves, and the discontented** — a shadow-reputation with its own uses.
- **Lifting the sentence:** lesser outlawry ends by time served. Full outlawry ends only by *royal pardon, lawful killing of a worse outlaw, or a deed so great the Thing itself votes you back* — the GM sets the price, and it should feel like a saga-ending.

## Teaching Beat

Outlawry teaches the value of the law by *removing* it. Players who have lived three sessions as the hunted will never again treat the Thing as set-dressing — they will understand, physically, what *friðr* was: the warm circle, and the cold outside it.

## At an OSE Table

- Lesser outlawry: three years banished, property forfeit, −3 Orðstír until lawful return, Hamingja capped at 5, kin shelter once per year — **identical**, the exile is system-neutral.
- Full outlawry: no frith, killable without weregild, no knowing shelter on pain of sharing the sentence — **identical**; additionally no hirelings will serve you and no stronghold will take you in.
- Exposure: **Save vs Death** each week in the wilds without proper shelter, or gain one exhaustion level; the rising cost (+1 per consecutive week, doubled in winter) stays exactly as written.
- Bounty: the GM sets a silver bounty — paid in **gp**; bounty-hunters as wandering encounters, fair-fight kills earning no debt but +1 Orðstír among the discontented — identical.
- Lifting the sentence: **identical** — time served for the lesser; royal pardon, a worse outlaw's lawful death, or a saga-ending deed voted home by the Thing for the full.
