## Transcript

> The first stanzas of Hávamál are not poetry — they are a manual. Fire, food, and clothes for the frozen traveler; water, towels for the feaster. The host must offer it all — a host who withholds it loses honor. Guests have duties: after three days, the host may name your leaving day; do not boast; keep weapons peace-bound under the roof. Harm a guest, and your honor is annihilated. In a land of winter, the door you close might be your own.

## The Rule (from HOMEBREW_CODEX.md §VIII)

- The **guest-rite**: when PCs arrive as guests, the host *must* offer fire, food/drink, and a seat — roleplayed, not handwaved. A host who performs it well gains +1 Orðstír; a host who withholds it loses 1, and the guests gain a *grievance claim*.
- **The guest's duties:** must not overstay — after three days, the host may name a departure day without insult; must not boast (boasting triggers the braga-goblet rule, §IV); must keep weapons peace-bound under the host's roof.
- **Guest-right violation** (harming a guest, or a guest harming the host) is a **hearth-frith breach** — the gravest circle. The violator's name is *spoken* at the next Thing; no weregild can be refused.
- **The wandering rule:** a PC traveling alone in winter may claim guest-right at any steading — survival *required* it, and the table learns why the sagas are full of strangers at doors.

## Teaching Beat

Players feel, in their bones, why hospitality was law: in a land of winter and wilderness, the door you close might be your own, next year.
