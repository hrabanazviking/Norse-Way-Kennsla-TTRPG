## The Rule (from HOMEBREW_CODEX.md §XXV)

- The land was *inhabited* — by **landvættir**, the spirits of place, who followed great men in crowds and who were owed respect: ships were to unship their carved figureheads before making landfall, lest the sight frighten the spirits. The dead, too, remained neighbors: the mound was a dwelling, and the unquiet dead — the **draugr** — walked when burial was denied, rites were skipped, or the dead had unfinished business. The lesson of both: *the dead are a maintenance obligation*.
- **Honoring the landvættir** (when entering new territory, founding a farm, or before a voyage): a small offering — food, drink poured out, a silver bit — and spoken greeting. Cost: trivial. Effect: **+1 Hamingja**, and the GM notes the land as *friendly* (shelter is easier to find, weather-rolls tilt kindly). Skipping it where custom demands: **−1 Hamingja** and the land as *cold* (the GM is entitled to one petty misfortune — a lame horse, a spoiled cache).
- **Insulting the landvættir** (fouling a holy spring, cutting a sacred grove, raising a níðstöng *falsely* on their ground): **−2 Hamingja** and a **haunting** — the GM runs minor phenomena (livestock sicken, tools break, sleep is troubled: no benefit from one long rest in seven) until atonement is made: a proper blót at the site (§IV) plus a gift of real value.
- **The unquiet dead:** any death without proper rites (§XIV) — no funeral, no remembrance-cup, the body left unburied — risks the dead *walking*. The GM rolls secretly: on **1–2 on 1d6**, the dead returns as a **draugr** (use the wight or ghast stat block, SRD, reskinned — bloated, grave-cold, impossibly strong). A draugr is not a random monster: it has *business* (guarding its goods, punishing its killer, finishing its feud), and it cannot be permanently laid until the business is settled *and* the rites are performed. Killing it twice without rites just makes it angrier.
- **Ancestor maintenance** (the reward side): keeping a family's mound, drinking minni to the dead at sumbel (§XIX), and speaking their names at the Thing: each observed season of this grants the household **+1 Hamingja** (the luck-line, §XVI) — the dead, properly kept, *lend* their luck to the living. Neglect for a full year: **−1 Hamingja** and restless dreams (§XXIII) until the mound is tended.

## Teaching Beat

The landscape becomes *populated* — every hill potentially watched, every ancestor a creditor. Players learn the animist economy: the world is full of persons, most of them not human, and all of them keep accounts.
