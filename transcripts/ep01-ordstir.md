# The Norse Way — Episode 1: Orðstír (Honor as Spendable Currency)

**Video:** `videos/ep01-ordstir.mp4` · 1080×1920 · ~30s · Narration: Unnr (Rumi voice)

## Transcript

> In the Viking Age, honor wasn't a feeling — it was money. We call it orðstír, word-fame: your reputation, rated zero to twenty, written where the whole table can see it. Do a brave deed before witnesses, gain a point. Spend it, one for one, as a bonus on any roll where your name matters. Die well, and half your honor passes to your heir. Remember: cattle die, kinsmen die — but a good name never dies.

## The Rule (from HOMEBREW_CODEX.md §I)

- Orðstír 0–20, all PCs begin at 5, tracked publicly at the table.
- Gain +1 for open deeds before witnesses (courage, generosity, wise counsel, oath kept at cost, praise-poems, holmgang victory, lavish hospitality).
- Lose −1 to −3 for cowardice, oathbreaking, stealthy theft, unanswered insult, miserliness, killing a yielded foe.
- Spend 1-for-1 as a bonus on Charisma checks where reputation matters; spent points return only through new deeds.
- At 0: níðingr-adjacent — no followers, no believed oaths. At 15+: saga-famous.
- Dying well: half your Orðstír passes to your named heir.

## Teaching Beat

Reputation is the real hit points. Players perform generosity and courage *because the mechanics reward being seen doing it* — exactly how the culture worked.
