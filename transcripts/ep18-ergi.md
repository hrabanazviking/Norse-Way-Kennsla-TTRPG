## The Rule (from HOMEBREW_CODEX.md §XVIII)

- **Ergi** meant unmanliness in its fullest, ugliest sense: cowardice in battle *and* sexual shame — the passive, the yielding, the perverse as the culture defined it. It was the **one accusation a man could not let stand**. The law treated the ergi-words (*ragr*, *argr*, and the acts they implied) as the deepest insults known.
- An **ergi-accusation** is a formal flyting charge (§X): it may be leveled only **in public, before witnesses, naming the act** — fleeing battle and leaving comrades; refusing holmgang when lawfully challenged; using seiðr or trolldom to *coerce* rather than to counsel; striking from hiding what should be faced openly.
- The accused must **answer within the scene**: by deed (a challenge met, a wrong righted before witnesses), by holmgang (§XI), or by a counter-flyting the table judges the stronger. **Silence, flight, or a weak answer = 1 níð-mark + −2 Hamingja**, immediately.
- A *proven* ergi-act (witnessed cowardice, witnessed coercive magic): **1 níð-mark, −2 Orðstír, −1 Hamingja** — and the word follows the character: NPCs who have heard it react with **open contempt until atonement**.
- **The seiðr nuance** (§V): a man practicing seiðr for prophecy, healing, or counsel at need suffers **no ergi-taint**; a man using it to unman a rival, to bind another's will, or to strike unseen **does**. The table judges intent — and the GM makes the judgment *public*, because the culture did. Óðinn practiced seiðr and remained Óðinn: the shame lay not in the art but in *how and why* a man used it.

## Teaching Beat

Players encounter a moral world that is not theirs — where courage is gendered, where *how* you fight matters as much as *whether* you win, and where the worst thing is not cruelty but *shameful* cruelty. The discomfort is the lesson: the 9th century is a foreign country, and the mechanics are its customs officers.
