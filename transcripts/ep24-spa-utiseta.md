## The Rule (from HOMEBREW_CODEX.md §XXIV)

- The **völva's spá** — the prophecy spoken from the high seat — is the oldest recorded voice in the tradition. *Spá* was not fortune-telling as entertainment; it was *consultation with consequence*. The seeker came with a real question and paid a real price.
- **Asking the spá** (a völva NPC, or a PC with the seiðr-path, §V): the seeker states *one* question aloud, before witnesses. The völva enters trance (an hour; the hall waits — play the waiting). The GM answers with a **riddle-answer**: true, but veiled (a verse, an image, a kenning-chain). The answer is *never* a plan — it is a weather-report of wyrd.
- **The price** (this is the point — fate does not do free samples):
  - *The small price:* a gift worthy of the answer — silver, a weapon, a service named on the spot (§VI). Stingy payment = muddled answer (the GM gives two meanings, one true — as §XXIII).
  - *The wyrd-price:* after any spá, the seeker gains **+1 Wyrd-debt** (§III). Knowing the shape of doom binds you tighter to it. The völva herself takes on spiritual risk — which is why wise völur sometimes *refuse* to answer, and the culture respected the refusal.
  - *The forbidden questions:* asking *when* you die, or demanding the gods change what is woven — the völva answers with silence, and the seeker loses **1 Hamingja** for the presumption. Some threads are not for pulling.
- **Útiseta** (a PC-declared night rite): the character sits out alone from dusk to dawn at a mound, shore, or crossroads, fasting. **Constitution save DC 13** at dawn or gain one level of **exhaustion** (the night is cold and the spirits are not kind). Success — truly sitting the whole night, no sleep, no leaving: the GM grants **one true dream-image** (§XXIII) or a whispered answer to a single question, at the cost of **+1 Wyrd-debt**. Útiseta may not be repeated for the same question — fate does not enjoy being nagged, and the second sitting brings only wind.

## Teaching Beat

Divination becomes *expensive*, which teaches why the culture rationed it. Players learn to save their one question for the one that matters — and to fear the answer slightly, which is the correct relationship with prophecy.
