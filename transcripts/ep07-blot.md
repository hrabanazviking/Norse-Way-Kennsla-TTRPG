# The Norse Way — Episode 7: Blót (Sacrifice for the Gods' Favor)

## Transcript

> The gods never appear — but they are owed. At a blót we gather with ale and cattle; blood is sprinkled on altar and people; the meat is feasted. Goblets cross the fire: Óðinn's for victory, Njörðr's and Freyja's for good seasons, the braga-goblet over which vows are sworn. Give, so the gods give back. They answer only in signs — ravens circling, a sudden wind, ale souring in the cup. Stint on the offering, and the omens turn.

## The Rule (from HOMEBREW_CODEX.md §IV)

- A blót needs: a proper place (hof, grove, vé, burial mound), an offering of real value (recorded in silver weight), and witnesses. The goði or eldest leads.
- The **braga-goblet** round is where oaths and boasts are sworn aloud — boasting and failing costs Orðstír equal to the boast's boldness.
- After the blót, the GM rolls the **Omen table** (d20): never a god appearing — ravens circling widdershins, a sudden fair wind, ale souring in the cup. A great offering grants advantage on the omen roll; a stingy one, disadvantage.
- The three great seasonal blóts (Yule, Dísablót, Sigrblót) follow the saga pattern.

## Teaching Beat

Players learn *reciprocity as cosmology*: you do not pray for favors, you *exchange* with powers greater than you — and the answer comes as weather and birds, which trains them to read the world as alive.

## At an OSE Table

- Omen roll: a great offering lets you **roll twice and take the better omen**; a stingy one, **roll twice and take the worse**.
- Everything else is identical — the gods take silver in any century.
