## The Rule (from HOMEBREW_CODEX.md §XXVIII)

- **Óðal** was not mere property — it was *inheritance made sacred*: the ancestral land held by a kin-group since time out of mind, inalienable except by the kin's consent, and reclaimable by kin even after sale (the odal right of redemption). When Haraldr Fairhair seized the óðal-lands of Norway's chieftains for the crown, the outrage drove whole families to Iceland — the settlement of a new country began as a protest against stolen inheritance [Heim: Haralds saga hárfagra]. To hold óðal was to *be* somebody; to lose it was to become nobody's son.
- **Óðal holdings** (for land-owning PCs or their kin): record the farm as a **character of its own** — name, acres, livestock, and an **Óðal-rating** 1–5 (prosperity + ancestral depth). Each year held and worked: **+1 Orðstír** for the head of the household (stewardship is honor), and the household gains **+1 Hamingja** (§XVI) — the luck-line lives in the soil.
- **Threats to the óðal** (the campaign engine): a jarl's tax collectors, a rival kin's claim at the Thing, a king's "redistribution," foreclosure by debt. Defending the óðal — by law (§VII), by payment, or by force — is the highest-stakes play available: success grants **+2 Orðstír** and **+1 Hamingja** (the ancestors approve); failure or surrender without a fight: **−2 Orðstír, −1 Hamingja**, and the kin-group's oath-helpers (§XXVI) shrink by half — who follows a man who would not fight for his father's acres?
- **The redemption right:** if óðal is sold under duress, any kinsman may **redeem** it within the customary term by repaying the price + a weregild-scale fee (`weregild.json`). A PC who redeems lost family land: **+3 Orðstír** — the single largest honorable gain outside a sumbel-vow kept, because the culture counted nothing higher than winning back what the fathers held.
- **New land** (for the landless — Iceland, the Danelaw, the east): clearing and holding new acres for three winters *creates* óðal. The first generation earns it the hard way: each winter survived on the new land, **+1 Orðstír**. The mechanic makes colonization a *moral* project, as the settlers saw it.

## Teaching Beat

Land stops being "real estate" and becomes *identity with a deed*. Players who have fought for a farm's name will understand, suddenly and completely, why men crossed an ocean over a tax dispute.
