## Transcript

> The Norse never thought of fate as a story — it was a weave you were caught in. Three Norns — Urðr, Verðandi, Skuld — carve your ørlög at birth. But fate names the price, never the choice. Your doom may be fixed; how you meet it is yours. Die in battle, oaths kept, weapon in hand, and you earn a saga-death: half your honor passed to your heir. Straw-death was the dread. A good death was the crown.

## The Rule (from HOMEBREW_CODEX.md §III)

- At character creation, the GM draws the PC's **birth rune** (Elder Futhark) — an omen, not a stat: the GM may invoke it once per session (advantage on one fitting roll, or a whispered warning).
- **Wyrd-debt** (oathbreaking, frith-breaking, coercive magic) accumulates silently. At 5, the GM performs a **Norn's reading**: three runes drawn openly as a doom the PC cannot dodge — only *meet well*. Clearing it takes a great public deed or a blót of real cost.
- **Dying well** (battle, oaths kept, kin protected, weapon in hand): a **saga-death** — a verse composed, half your Orðstír to your heir, and your *next* character begins with Inspiration and +2 Orðstír, "born under a bright name."
- Straw-death, cowardice, oathbreaking: no verse, no inheritance, and the table knows it.
- Wyrd names the price; it never forces the choice.

## Teaching Beat

Players stop fearing character death and start fearing *meaningless* death — the exact inversion of modern play instincts, and the heart of the Norse worldview.

## At an OSE Table

- Birth-rune invocation: **−2 on a roll-under check** (instead of advantage) on one fitting roll per session, or the whispered warning.
- Saga-death: your next character begins with **one free reroll** (instead of Inspiration) and +2 Orðstír.
- Everything else is identical — wyrd is wyrd in any century.
