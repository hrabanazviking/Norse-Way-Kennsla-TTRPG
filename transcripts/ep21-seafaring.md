## The Rule (from HOMEBREW_CODEX.md §XIII)

- **No teleportation, no fantasy navigation aids.** A voyage is planned in *day's sails* between known landmarks — use the realm's real geography (Birka to Hedeby, Kaupang to Dublin; see canon location tables). Navigation was *embodied knowledge*: sun and stars, landmarks, the color and smell of water, bird flight, whale roads, sounding-lead.
- **The sailing check:** each day's sail, the steersman rolls **Wisdom (Survival)** — advantage if the crew includes a seasoned *leiðsögumaðr* (a pilot who knows these waters) or if landmarks are visible; disadvantage in fog, storm, or unknown waters. Failure is not instantly "lost at sea" — it is **hafvilla**: off course, supplies burning, the GM rolling secretly for where the wind has taken them.
- **Reading the sea** (any crew member, once per day): **Intelligence (Nature)** — bird flight at dusk (landward), whale spouts (deep water), water color, cloud banks over unseen islands. Success gives the steersman advantage on the next sailing check. *This* is the Norse GPS.
- **Storms are not random encounters — they are wyrd.** When a storm comes the crew chooses: run before it (lose course, maybe ship), beach the ship (lose time, save lives), or ride it out (Constitution saves vs. exhaustion, ship damage). There is no "control weather" shortcut at this table — even seiðr weather-working takes hours of ritual and only *asks* the wind.
- **Beaching:** ships are hauled ashore each night where possible; a ship left anchored unwatched may be cut loose — and a ship is worth more than a farm.

## Teaching Beat

Players learn navigation as *literacy* — reading birds, water, and sky — and feel in their gut why the Norse called the sea the *whale-road*: it was a road you had to know by heart.
