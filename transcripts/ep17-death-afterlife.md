# The Norse Way — Episode 17: Death & the Afterlife (What a Good Death Buys)

**Video:** `videos/ep17-death-afterlife.mp4` · 1080×1920 · ~30s · Narration: Unnr (Rumi voice)

## Transcript

> The Norse did not share one afterlife — they shared a geography of the dead. The battle-slain, chosen by the valkyries, go to Valhöll or Freyja's Fólkvangr. The drowned fall into Rán's net. The ordinary dead — the straw-dead, the sick, the old — rest in Helheim's quiet halls: no punishment, only dimness. How you died routed you; your funeral rites equipped you. At this table, a good death is authorship — the player writes the ending the way skalds wrote endings.

## The Rule (from HOMEBREW_CODEX.md §XIV)

- **Death rites are played, not skipped:** the table holds a short funeral — burning or mounding, goods laid with the dead (recorded; they *leave the party's wealth* — the dead take their share, as is right), a remembrance goblet drunk at the next blót (§IV). Skipping the rites risks the dead walking: *draugr* are not monsters here — they are **unfuneraled obligations** (§II's *dauðrafriðr*, frith with the dead).
- **Routing** (told in-world by the goði, believed absolutely): slain in battle with weapon in hand, chosen → **Valhöll** or **Fólkvangr** (the valkyries' choosing is the GM's secret — the table never knows which, and it doesn't matter); drowned at sea → **Rán's net** — feared: a silver offering before sailing grants **advantage on one storm-save**; straw-death, sickness, age → **Helheim's quiet halls** — *not* a place of punishment but a vast, quiet realm of diminished existence; not shameful for the old, but a young warrior's dread. Oathbreakers and *níðingar* → the shore of corpses, **Náströnd** — present it as *priestly warning*, not verified geography.
- **The good death** (§III): dying well banks legacy — saga-verse, inherited Orðstír, the next character's bright beginning. The table *celebrates* a good death the way other tables celebrate a level-up. That celebration *is* the lesson.

## Teaching Beat

Death stops being failure and becomes *authorship*: the player writes their character's ending the way the skalds wrote endings — and learns why a culture that believed this feared the bed more than the battlefield.

## At an OSE Table

- The funeral rite is **identical** — burning or mounding, goods recorded and leaving the party's wealth, remembrance goblet at the next blót; skip it and the dead may walk.
- Routing is **identical**, believed absolutely in-world: Valhöll/Fólkvangr for the battle-slain (the GM's secret), Rán's net for the drowned, Helheim's quiet halls for the straw-dead, Náströnd as priestly warning for oathbreakers.
- Rán's offering: a silver offering before sailing grants **+2 on one storm-save** (or a re-roll, table's choice).
- The good death: saga-verse, inherited Orðstír, and the next character's bright beginning — **identical**; the table celebrates it either way.
