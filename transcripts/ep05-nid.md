# The Norse Way — Episode 5: Níð & the Níðingr (Shame as a Weapon)

**Video:** `videos/ep05-nid.mp4` · 1080×1920 · ~30s · Narration: Unnr (Rumi voice)

## Transcript

> In this culture, shame is a weapon with legal standing. It's called níð: cowardice, treachery, the unspeakable. Earn three níð-marks and you're níðingr — socially dead. Your oath is worthless, your luck is capped, and the law may let you be killed without weregild. Or raise the níðstöng: a horse's head on a pole, carved with curses, turned at your enemy — and the land-spirits themselves drive him out.

## The Rule (from HOMEBREW_CODEX.md §XVII)

- Níð-marks: certain acts earn a mark (announced by the GM, witnessed by the table). Three marks = níðingr. Marks fade only by public atonement: a great witnessed deed, or holmgang vindication.
- Níðingr status: your oath is worthless (automatic failure on honor-weighed Persuasion; no oath-helpers at the Thing); you may be killed without weregild (the world's belief — a threat, rarely an event); Hamingja cannot rise above 3; redemption is possible but costly.
- Raising the níðstöng (never casual): horse's head or carved likeness, a pole, and a spoken/carved níð-verse (the player speaks at least a couplet aloud). The target loses 2 Orðstír among all who hear of it, and the landvættir turn — −1 Hamingja until the pole is answered or avenged. Raising a pole falsely earns the raiser 2 níð-marks.

## Teaching Beat

Words are weapons with legal standing. Shame here is not a feeling — it is a status, and statuses have teeth.
